✈️ Final Exit or Exit Re-Entry? Choose Right Before You Book That Flight

One keeps your job and iqama waiting for you. The other closes the door, though not forever. Here's how to tell them apart and avoid a very expensive mix-up.

Two visas, two very different futures. Pick the right one and you're on a holiday with your job waiting for you. Pick the wrong one and you might land at home to find your iqama has been cancelled. It happens more often than you'd think, usually because someone ticked the wrong box in a hurry.

Short answer: an exit re-entry visa is for a trip: you leave, come back, and your iqama and job carry on. A final exit visa is for leaving for good: your iqama is cancelled and your residency ends. You can still come back later on a brand-new visa, and there's no ban if you left properly.

Side by Side

Exit re-entryFinal exit
PurposeHoliday, family visit, short tripLeaving Saudi Arabia permanently
Your iqamaStays valid; you return to the same sponsorCancelled when you leave

ValidityThe period you pay for; your iqama must stay valid throughoutUsually 60 days to leave, capped by your iqama's expiry
Who issues itYour employer (Muqeem / Absher Business); you for your dependants (Absher)Your employer, or you through Qiwa once your contract has ended
Coming backOn the same iqama, before the visa expiresOnly with a new work or visit visa

Which One Is Right for You?

  • Going home on holiday and coming back to the same job: exit re-entry. Price it with our exit re-entry calculator.
  • Resigned or contract ended, with no new Saudi job: final exit.
  • Resigned with a new Saudi job lined up: neither. Your new employer transfers your iqama through Qiwa. See what happens to your iqama if you resign.
  • Sending your family home for good while you stay: final exit for them, issued by you on Absher. Your own iqama is unaffected.

Example: Two Colleagues, Two Visas

Aisha is flying to Manila for her brother's wedding and will be away five weeks. Her employer issues a single exit re-entry for two months for SAR 200. She checks her iqama is valid for the whole trip, flies on the 3rd and is back at her desk on the 9th of the following month.

Daniel has finished a five-year contract and is moving back to Nairobi. His employer pays his final settlement, he clears a SAR 300 traffic fine, and his final exit is issued. He flies 10 days later. Two years on, a Jeddah company offers him a job, and he returns on a new work visa with no ban and no problems.

Before a Final Exit: Clear These First

  • Traffic fines and unpaid dependent fees must be paid, or the visa can't be issued.
  • Your family on your iqama needs final exit visas too, and should leave before you or with you.
  • Loans and credit cards: settle them. Unpaid debts can lead to a travel ban through the courts.
  • Final settlement: make sure your end of service, unused leave and last salary are paid, or a complaint is filed, before you fly. Check them with our final settlement calculator.
  • Bank and GOSI: close or update your bank account, and download your GOSI statement and salary certificate.

A final exit not used within its validity can lapse, with a fine before it's cancelled. Book your flight as soon as it's issued.

On an Exit Re-Entry: Don't Overstay Abroad

Return before the visa expires. If you can't, your employer may be able to extend it online while you're abroad, as long as your iqama is still valid.

The old rule that barred workers who didn't return for three years was lifted by Jawazat in January 2024. Someone whose exit re-entry expired abroad can now come back on a new visa, with their previous employer or a new one. One thing hasn't changed: an exit re-entry can't be converted into a final exit while you're outside the Kingdom. If you're leaving for good, sort out the final exit before you fly.

Mistakes People Make

  • Taking a final exit "just to be safe" before a holiday. It cancels your iqama, and your job goes with it.
  • Leaving on exit re-entry when you're quitting for good. You can't switch it to final exit from abroad, and your old employer is left with loose ends.
  • Letting the iqama expire mid-trip. Exit re-entry dates can't run past your residency.
  • Forgetting the family. Dependants need their own visas, either way.
  • Flying before your settlement is paid. Chasing it from abroad is much harder.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between final exit and exit re-entry?
Exit re-entry is for a trip: your iqama stays valid and you return to the same employer. Final exit ends your residency and cancels your iqama.
How much is an exit re-entry visa?
A single exit re-entry costs SAR 200 for up to 2 months, then SAR 100 for each extra month. A multiple exit re-entry costs SAR 500 for up to 3 months, then SAR 200 for each extra month.
How long is a final exit visa valid?
Usually 60 days to leave, capped by your iqama's expiry date.
Can I come back to Saudi Arabia after final exit?
Yes. There's no ban after a normal final exit. You need a new work or visit visa to return.
Is there still a 3-year ban if I don't return on exit re-entry?
No. Jawazat lifted that ban in January 2024. You can return on a new visa, with your previous employer or a new one.
Can I change my exit re-entry to final exit while abroad?
No. An exit re-entry can't be converted into a final exit while you're outside Saudi Arabia, so arrange the final exit before you fly.
