Your Company Is Closing Down: What Happens to You?

Closures and bankruptcies happen, and workers are often the last to know. Here is what you are owed and how to protect it.

Updated November 2026Saudi Labour LawFor expat employees
Home › Guides › Company Closure

Short answer: if your employer closes, your contract ends because of the employer, not because you resigned. That means you are owed your full end-of-service award under Article 84, plus unpaid salary and unused annual leave. You can move to a new employer through Qiwa without the old company's approval. If the company is insolvent, workers' wages and end-of-service awards are given priority over most other debts.

What You Are Owed

ItemHow it is calculated
End-of-service awardFull Article 84 award: half a month's wage for each of the first five years, one month's wage for each year after. No resignation reduction.
Unpaid salaryEvery day worked up to the last day
Unused annual leave(Monthly wage ÷ 30) × days left
Notice payIf the contract was ended without the required notice
Return ticketIf your contract includes one

Example: 6 years of service on a SAR 9,000 wage gives 2.5 + 1 = 3.5 months, so SAR 31,500 in end of service, before salary and leave. Check your own figure with the end of service calculator or look it up in our EOS payout tables.

Payment Deadline

When the employer ends the contract, final dues must be paid within one week of the last day. If that passes, file a claim through Qiwa straight away. Don't wait for the company to "sort things out", because closing companies often run out of money fast.

Your Iqama: Move Quickly

  • Once your contract ends you have a limited window, commonly 60 days, to transfer to a new employer or leave on final exit.
  • A transfer is started by the new employer through Qiwa. Your old employer's approval is not needed once the contract has ended.
  • If the company stops renewing work permits or is suspended, some services become blocked, so start job-hunting before the closure date if you can.

If the Company Won't or Can't Pay

  1. File a labour claim on Qiwa for each item: wages, end of service, leave. See our complaint guide.
  2. If the company has entered formal bankruptcy proceedings, your claim joins the list of creditors, with wage and end-of-service claims ranked ahead of most ordinary debts.
  3. Join other employees. Several workers filing the same claim with the same evidence is efficient and hard to ignore.
Do not sign a settlement for less than you are owed under pressure of "take it or get nothing". Ask the settlement officer what the court process would give you first.

Saudi Nationals: SANED

Saudi employees who lose their jobs for reasons outside their control may also be eligible for SANED unemployment insurance through GOSI. Expatriates are not covered by SANED.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I get full end of service if my company closes?
Yes. A closure ends the contract because of the employer, so the full Article 84 award applies, not the reduced resignation share.
How soon must a closing company pay final dues?
Within one week of the last day of the contract when the employer ends it.
Can I transfer my iqama if my company closes?
Yes. Once the contract ends, a new employer can transfer you through Qiwa without the old employer's approval.
What if the company goes bankrupt?
Your claims join the bankruptcy proceedings, where wages and end-of-service awards are ranked ahead of most ordinary debts. File your claim early.
