🏢 Your Company Is Closing Down: How to Protect Every Riyal You're Owed

Closures and bankruptcies happen, and workers are often the last to know. Here's exactly what you're owed, and why moving fast matters.

💰 Full Article 84 award⏱️ One-week deadline🔄 Transfer without approval
Home › Guides › Company Closure

It usually starts with rumours: a delayed salary, managers in closed-door meetings, a supplier turning up at reception. Then the email arrives: the company is closing. Your first thoughts are your iqama, your end of service and the months of salary you might never see.

Take a breath. A closure is one of the situations where the law is firmly on your side. You just have to move faster than the company's money runs out.

Short answer: if your employer closes, your contract ends because of the employer, not because you resigned. You're owed your full end-of-service award under Article 84, plus unpaid salary and unused leave. You can move to a new employer through Qiwa without the old company's approval. If the company is insolvent, wages and end-of-service awards rank ahead of most other debts.

What You're Owed

ItemHow it's calculated
End-of-service awardFull Article 84 award: half a month's wage for each of the first five years, one month for each year after that, with no resignation reduction
Unpaid salaryEvery day worked up to the last day
Unused annual leave(Monthly wage ÷ 30) × days left
Notice payIf the contract was ended without the required notice (60 days on an open-ended contract)
Return ticketIf your contract includes one

Real Example: Anil's Closing Notice

Anil has worked six years for a Riyadh contracting firm on SAR 9,000 a month. In September the company announces it will close at the end of October, with no notice pay offered. Here's his bill:

  • End of service: 2.5 + 1 = 3.5 months × 9,000 = SAR 31,500
  • Unpaid October salary: SAR 9,000
  • 12 days of unused leave: 9,000 ÷ 30 × 12 = SAR 3,600
  • Notice pay if the 60 days weren't given: up to SAR 18,000

That's more than SAR 60,000, nearly seven months' salary. Anil lists every item in an email to HR on his last day. When nothing arrives within a week, he files on Qiwa the same afternoon, alongside eleven colleagues with the same claim.

The One-Week Deadline

When the employer ends the contract, final dues must be paid within one week of the last day. If that passes, file a claim through Qiwa straight away. Don't wait for the company to "sort things out". Closing companies often run out of money fast, and early claims are easier to enforce.

Your Iqama: Move Quickly

  • Once the contract ends you have a limited window, commonly 60 days, to transfer to a new employer or leave on final exit.
  • A transfer is started by the new employer through Qiwa. Your old employer's approval isn't needed once the contract has ended.
  • If the company stops renewing work permits or gets suspended, some services become blocked. Start job-hunting before the closure date if you can.
  • Check your iqama expiry date now. A closing company is unlikely to renew it.

If the Company Won't, or Can't, Pay

  • File a labour claim on Qiwa for each item: wages, end of service, leave, notice. Our complaint guide explains the steps.
  • Formal bankruptcy? Your claim joins the list of creditors, with wage and end-of-service claims ranked ahead of most ordinary debts. Register it early.
  • Join forces with colleagues. Many workers filing the same claim with the same evidence is efficient and hard to ignore.
  • Don't take "take it or get nothing" deals under pressure. Ask the settlement officer what the court process would give you first.

Mistakes People Make

  • Resigning when you hear the rumours. Resigning turns a full award into a reduced one. Let the company end the contract.
  • Waiting months for a promised payment. File after the one-week deadline passes.
  • Leaving on final exit before claiming. Following a case from abroad is much harder.
  • Losing your paperwork. Download your Qiwa contract, payslips and GOSI statement before access disappears.

Your Closure Checklist for This Week

  1. Download your Qiwa contract, last six payslips and your GOSI statement.
  2. Write down your exact start date, last working day and unused leave days.
  3. Email HR a list of what you're owed, item by item, and keep the reply.
  4. Ask in writing whether notice pay will be paid, and when.
  5. Update your CV and tell recruiters you're available, so your 60 days aren't wasted.
  6. Diary the date one week after your last day. If nothing has arrived, that's when you file.

Saudi Nationals: SANED

Saudi employees who lose their jobs for reasons outside their control may also qualify for SANED unemployment insurance through GOSI. Expats aren't covered by SANED, but every right above applies to them in full.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I get full end of service if my company closes?
Yes. A closure ends the contract because of the employer, so the full Article 84 award applies, not the reduced resignation share.
How soon must a closing company pay final dues?
Within one week of the last day of the contract when the employer ends it. If it doesn't, file on Qiwa straight away.
Can I transfer my iqama if my company closes?
Yes. Once the contract ends, a new employer can transfer you through Qiwa without the old employer's approval.
What if the company goes bankrupt?
Your claims join the bankruptcy proceedings, where wages and end-of-service awards rank ahead of most ordinary debts. Register your claim early.
Should I resign if I hear my company is closing?
Usually not. Resigning reduces your end-of-service award. Let the company end the contract so you keep the full award and notice pay.
