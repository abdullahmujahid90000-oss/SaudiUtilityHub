Salary Not Paid? What To Do in Saudi Arabia

A delayed salary is stressful, especially with rent, school fees and family abroad depending on it. Here are your options, in the order that protects you best.

Updated November 2026Saudi Labour LawFor expat employees
Home › Guides › Unpaid Salary

Short answer: monthly-paid workers must be paid at least once a month, through a bank, under the Wage Protection System. If your salary is late, raise it in writing, then file a complaint on Qiwa. If wages stay unpaid, the law gives you strong options: you can usually move to a new employer without your current employer's approval, and under Article 81 you can leave without notice and keep your full end-of-service award.

Your Basic Rights

  • Monthly pay: workers on a monthly wage must be paid at least once a month.
  • Bank transfer: salaries go through the Wage Protection System (WPS), so the ministry can see when an employer stops paying.
  • No unlawful deductions: an employer can only deduct for limited reasons set by law, such as recovering an advance, and within limits.
  • Full pay for days worked: even if you leave, the employer owes your salary up to your last day.

What To Do, Step by Step

  1. Document it. Screenshot bank statements showing the missing months and keep your payslips.
  2. Ask in writing. Email HR with the months and amounts and a clear date to pay. Keep working while you wait.
  3. File a complaint on Qiwa for unpaid wages. See our step-by-step complaint guide. Filing creates an official record before you take any bigger step.
  4. Choose your next move, from the options below.

Option A: Transfer to a New Employer

Under the labour mobility rules introduced in 2021, there are cases where a worker can transfer to a new employer through Qiwa without the current employer's approval. One of the commonly listed cases is when wages have not been paid for three consecutive months. Your new employer submits the transfer request in Qiwa, and the system checks the conditions.

Line up the new job first. The transfer is started by the new employer, not by you.

Option B: Leave Under Article 81 and Keep Your Rights

Article 81 of the Labour Law lets a worker leave without notice while keeping all legal rights when the employer fails to meet its essential obligations, and not paying wages is the clearest example. Because the employer caused the exit, the end-of-service award is treated like a termination, so you get the full award rather than the reduced resignation share.

How you leaveEnd of service with 4 years on SAR 8,000
Normal resignation (Article 85, one-third)SAR 5,333
Leaving under Article 81 (full award)SAR 16,000

To use Article 81 safely, send a written notice that names the breach (for example, "salaries for July to October 2026 unpaid") and says you are ending the contract under Article 81. Do this after filing your complaint, so the record shows why you stopped working. This protects you if the employer tries to report you as absent.

Option C: Stay and Claim

If you want to keep the job, you can stay and pursue only the unpaid wages through the complaint. Many cases settle at the amicable settlement stage once the ministry is involved.

What Not To Do

  • Don't just stop going to work without a complaint on file. That is how workers end up reported as absent.
  • Don't sign a final settlement or a "no further claims" paper until you have checked every amount.
  • Don't leave on final exit before your claim is filed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What can I do if my employer doesn't pay my salary in Saudi Arabia?
Raise it in writing, then file a complaint on Qiwa. If wages stay unpaid you can usually transfer to a new employer without approval, or leave under Article 81 and keep your full end-of-service award.
Can I transfer my iqama if my salary is not paid?
Yes, in many cases. Unpaid wages for three consecutive months is one of the commonly listed cases where the new employer can transfer you through Qiwa without your current employer's approval.
Do I lose end of service if I leave because my salary wasn't paid?
No. Leaving under Article 81 because the employer failed to pay keeps all your rights, including the full end-of-service award rather than the reduced resignation share.
Should I stop working when my salary is late?
Not before you have filed a complaint. Stopping without a record can lead to an absent-from-work report.
