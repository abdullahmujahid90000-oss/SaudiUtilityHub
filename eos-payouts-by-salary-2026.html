End of Service Payouts by Salary Band — Saudi Arabia 2026

How much gratuity does a worker in Saudi Arabia actually take home after 1, 5, 10 or 20 years? We calculated every combination of eight common salary bands and nine service lengths, for both termination and resignation.

Data study · October 2026 Source: Labour Law Art. 84 & 85 (HRSD) Free to cite · CSV download
Home › End of Service Calculator › EOS Payouts by Salary Band 2026

Key findings

  • Five years earns 2.5 months of wage, ten years earns 7.5 months. The first five years accrue at half a month per year; every year after that accrues a full month. A worker on SAR 10,000 who completes 10 years and is terminated or reaches contract end receives SAR 75,000.
  • Resigning one month too early can halve the payout. On a SAR 10,000 wage, resigning at 4 years 11 months pays about SAR 8,194; waiting one more month to reach 5 years pays about SAR 16,667, roughly twice as much.
  • The 10-year mark is the biggest cliff. At 9 years 11 months a resigning worker gets two-thirds of the award (about SAR 49,444 on SAR 10,000). At 10 years they get the full SAR 75,000, a jump of about SAR 25,500 for one extra month.
  • Under 2 years, resignation pays nothing. A resigning worker with 1 year 11 months receives SAR 0, while the same worker whose contract ends receives about 0.96 months of wage.

Worked examples

1. Contract ends after 7 years, wage SAR 8,000

First 5 years: 5 × 0.5 = 2.5 months. Next 2 years: 2 × 1 = 2 months. Total 4.5 months × SAR 8,000 = SAR 36,000.

2. Resignation after 3 years 6 months, wage SAR 5,000

Full award: 3.5 × 0.5 = 1.75 months = SAR 8,750. Resignation between 2 and 5 years pays one-third: SAR 2,916.67.

3. Resignation after 12 years, wage SAR 15,000

Full award: 2.5 + 7 = 9.5 months = SAR 142,500. At 10+ years a resigning worker receives the full amount: SAR 142,500.

Methodology

  • Formula. Article 84: half a month's wage for each of the first five years of service and one month's wage for each later year, with partial years pro-rated. Article 85: the resignation shares above. Both as published by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (HRSD) — End-of-Service Award Regulations.
  • Wage. "Monthly wage" means the last wage used for the award. HRSD defines it as basic wage plus other contractual amounts, so a fixed housing allowance is normally included. Figures scale linearly, so you can read off any wage by proportion.
  • Not included. Unpaid salary, leave encashment (see the Leave Calculator), notice-period compensation, Article 77 compensation for unlawful termination, and Article 80 dismissals (no award).
  • Rounding. Tables are rounded to the nearest riyal. The full dataset (CSV) keeps two decimals.
  • Last reviewed: 3 October 2026, against the HRSD page linked above.

Frequently asked questions

How much end of service will I get after 5 years in Saudi Arabia?

If your contract ends or your employer terminates you (not for an Article 80 reason), 5 years of service earns 2.5 months of your last wage: half a month for each of the first five years. On a SAR 10,000 wage that is SAR 25,000. If you resign at exactly 5 years you receive two-thirds of that, about SAR 16,667.

Do I get end of service if I resign before 2 years?

No. Under Article 85 of the Saudi Labour Law a worker who resigns with less than two years of continuous service receives no end-of-service award. Termination by the employer or the natural end of a fixed-term contract is different: Article 84 then applies from the first year, pro-rated.

Why does resigning one month before 5 or 10 years cost so much?

Article 85 pays resigning workers one-third of the award between 2 and 5 years, two-thirds between 5 and 10 years and the full award after 10 years. The share jumps at each threshold, so on a SAR 10,000 wage resigning at 4 years 11 months pays about SAR 8,194 while resigning at 5 years pays about SAR 16,667.

Which wage is used — basic or basic plus allowances?

Article 84 uses the last wage. HRSD defines wage as the basic wage plus other amounts due under the contract, so regular fixed allowances such as housing are normally included; only variable items like commissions may be excluded by agreement. The tables on this page apply to whichever monthly wage figure your contract uses.

Are these figures official?

They are calculated directly from the formulas in Articles 84 and 85 as published by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (HRSD). They are not survey data and do not include unpaid salary, leave encashment or notice pay. Check a binding figure with HRSD's own calculator or Qiwa.
