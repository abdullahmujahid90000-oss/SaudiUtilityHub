⏰ Notice Period in Saudi Arabia: 30 Days or 60? It Depends Who's Leaving

Since 2025 the rules depend on who ends the contract. Here's what you owe, what you're owed, and the pay-in-lieu maths, with real examples.

You've decided to move on, or your manager has just told you they're letting you go. Either way, the next question is the same: how much notice does each side owe? Get it wrong and someone ends up paying a month or two of salary they didn't need to.

Short answer: on an open-ended (unlimited) contract with a monthly wage, the minimum notice is 30 days if you resign and 60 days if the employer ends the contract. Workers not paid monthly give and get 30 days either way. Your contract can set longer periods. Whoever skips the notice owes the other side the wage for it.

Who Owes How Much Notice

SituationMinimum notice
You resign (open-ended contract, monthly wage)30 days
Employer ends the contract (open-ended, monthly wage)60 days
Worker not paid monthly30 days from either side
Fixed-term contractNo notice in the usual sense; ending early can mean compensation instead

These minimums come from the Labour Law amendments that took effect in February 2025. Before that, the rules were the same for both sides, which is why older articles (and some HR teams) still say "30 days each way".

When You Resign: The Acceptance Rules

  • Your employer has 30 days to respond to a written resignation. Silence counts as acceptance.
  • They can postpone it once, by up to 60 days, for a genuine work reason given to you in writing within those 30 days.
  • You can withdraw it within 7 days, provided it hasn't already been accepted.

For the residency side of leaving, read what happens to your iqama if you resign.

No Notice Given? Then It's Paid in Cash

A side that ends the contract without the required notice must pay the other side the wage for the notice period.

Example: Sara is let go with immediate effect. She earns SAR 7,500 a month on an open-ended contract. Her employer owes her 60 days' notice pay, which is SAR 15,000, on top of her end-of-service award, unused leave and final salary.

And the other way round: Khalid walks out. He leaves after a week of his 30-day notice. His employer can claim the wage for the notice he didn't serve, roughly three weeks' pay, and may deduct it from his final settlement.

Fixed-Term Contracts: Compensation, Not Notice

Most expat contracts are fixed-term. Since the February 2025 amendments, a worker on a fixed-term contract can resign through a formal route: submit it in writing and keep working while you wait. The employer has 30 days to accept, delay once by up to 60 days with a written reason, or stay silent (which counts as acceptance after 30 days). The 30-day and 60-day notice figures above apply to open-ended contracts, which are mainly held by Saudis. Check which type you have on Qiwa.

A fixed-term contract ends on its end date, so there's no notice period in the usual sense. If either side ends it early without a lawful reason, it may owe compensation, typically wages for the rest of the term, unless the contract sets a different amount. Before you resign mid-term, read the termination clause and do the maths. Sometimes waiting a few months is far cheaper.

The good news: if a fixed-term contract simply expires, that isn't a resignation. You get the full end-of-service award under Article 84, not the reduced resignation share.

Still on Probation? Different Rules

During probation (up to 180 days), either side can usually end the contract without notice, compensation or an end-of-service award, unless the contract says otherwise. If you're in your first months, check the dates with our Probation Calculator before you assume you owe 30 days.

Working out your last day: count from the day after your resignation is received. Resign in writing on 1 June with 30 days' notice and your last working day is 1 July. Put that date in the email so nobody can argue about it later.

What Happens During Your Notice Period

  • You're still employed: full salary, GOSI and service time all continue.
  • Your service keeps counting towards end of service, including the notice period itself. If the 2-, 5- or 10-year mark falls within it, that can change your payout.
  • Use the time to settle your final dues in writing. Check the numbers with our Final Settlement Calculator.
  • Line up your next step: a Qiwa transfer to a new employer, or a final exit. You'll have 60 days once the contract ends.

Mistakes People Make

  • Resigning verbally. Without a written date, nobody can say when your notice started.
  • Assuming 30 days both ways. Since 2025 the employer owes 60 days on an open-ended contract.
  • Ignoring the contract. Longer notice in your contract is binding on both sides.
  • Walking out early. Unserved notice can come out of your final settlement.
  • Forgetting the end-of-service cliffs. One more month of service can double a resignation payout. See our payout tables.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the notice period in Saudi Arabia?
On an open-ended contract with a monthly wage, at least 30 days if the worker resigns and 60 days if the employer ends the contract. For workers not paid monthly, it's 30 days from either side. Your contract can set longer periods.
Can my employer refuse my resignation?
Not indefinitely. They have 30 days to respond, and silence counts as acceptance. They can postpone it once by up to 60 days for a genuine work reason given in writing.
What if my employer fires me without notice?
They owe you the wage for the 60-day notice period, on top of your end of service, unused leave and other dues.
What if I leave without serving my notice?
Your employer can claim the wage for the notice you didn't serve, and may deduct it from your final settlement.
Is there a notice period on a fixed-term contract?
Not in the usual sense. Ending one early without a lawful reason can mean paying compensation for the rest of the term instead.
Does the notice period count towards end of service?
Yes. You're still employed during your notice, so it counts as service.
