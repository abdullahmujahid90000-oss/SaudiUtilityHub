👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family Visit Visa: Bring Your Loved Ones to Saudi Arabia

You've pictured it a hundred times: your mother at the arrivals gate in Riyadh, your kids running to their grandparents. Here's how to turn that picture into a visa, step by step, without the wasted trips and rejected uploads.

Every expat family has the same conversation sooner or later: "Why don't you come and stay with us for a few months?" The good news is that Saudi Arabia makes this easier than most people expect. You, the resident, apply online. Your family doesn't have to queue anywhere until they collect the visa or board the plane.

The short answer: the resident in Saudi Arabia applies through the MOFA Visa Platform (visa.mofa.gov.sa), signing in with Nafath. Close family (spouse, children and parents) are covered. The widely reported government fee is SAR 300, plus visitor health insurance. A visit lets them stay for a set period, usually up to 90 days at a time, and can often be extended through Absher while the visa is still valid.

What a Family Visit Visa Is (and What It Isn't)

Think of it as a long holiday pass for your relatives. It lets them live with you, travel around the Kingdom and, these days, perform Umrah too (book the permit through Nusuk, as our Umrah guide explains).

It is not a residence visa. Your visitors can't work, can't get an iqama on it, and can't stay indefinitely. If you want your spouse and children to live here with you full time, that's a different process. See our Family Residence Visa guide for that.

Who Can You Invite?

The family visit service is built for first-degree relatives:

  • Your spouse, proved with a marriage certificate
  • Your children, proved with birth certificates showing your name
  • Your parents, proved with your own birth certificate showing their names

Want to invite a brother, sister or in-law? Most guides place those relatives under a separate "personal visit" request rather than the family visit request. Pick the request type carefully on the platform, because choosing the wrong one is a common reason for rejection.

Your own situation matters too. The platform checks that your iqama is valid and that your profession (the job title on your iqama) is one allowed to request family visits. For some professions the request also has to be authenticated by your employer or the Chamber of Commerce before it moves forward.

How to Apply: Six Steps From Your Sofa

  1. Get the documents ready first. Relationship certificates, translated into Arabic and attested in the country that issued them. This is the slowest step by far, so start here.
  2. Check passports. Each visitor's passport should have at least six months left on it.
  3. Sign in to the MOFA Visa Platform at visa.mofa.gov.sa using Nafath. Choose Individuals → Resident → Family Visit Request.
  4. Fill in one request per visitor and upload the documents. Choose single or multiple entry.
  5. Get it authenticated by your employer or the Chamber of Commerce if your profession needs it, then pay the fee shown at checkout.
  6. Your family completes their side. Depending on their country, that may mean a stamping or biometrics appointment with the Saudi embassy or its visa-service partner. Then they book flights.

Complete requests are often decided within a few working days. Gathering the paperwork back home is what usually takes weeks, which is why step 1 comes first.

Fee figures differ between sources, with some quoting SAR 420–430 once service charges are added. The amount the platform shows you at checkout is the one that counts. To budget the whole trip, try our Family Visa Cost Calculator.

Entry Window, Stay and Extension: Three Different Dates

This is where families most often get caught out. Your visa carries three separate ideas:

  • Entry window: the dates during which your visitor must arrive.
  • Permitted stay: how long they may remain after they land. It's commonly 90 days per visit. A multiple-entry visa can be valid for a year, but each visit still has its own cap.
  • Extension: extra time requested through Absher (Visit Visa Extension) before the permitted stay runs out. Insurance has to cover the extra period too.

Sources disagree on the maximum total stay after extensions, with 180 days often quoted for a single-entry visa. Read the "duration of stay" printed on your visitor's visa and treat it as the deadline, not the date of the flight you hoped to book.

Real-Life Example: Imran's Parents Come for Ramadan

Imran is an accountant in Riyadh. In January he decides his parents in Lahore should spend Ramadan with his family.

  • January: his father collects Imran's birth certificate and has it attested and translated. That takes about three weeks.
  • Early February: Imran logs in with Nafath and submits two requests, one for each parent, choosing multiple entry. He pays SAR 300 each plus service charges.
  • Mid-February: both visas are issued. His parents buy visitor insurance and book flights for the first week of Ramadan.
  • Day 80: they decide to stay for Eid. Imran extends through Absher before day 90, after topping up their insurance.

The lesson is simple: start the paperwork early, apply once it's ready, and put the "leave by" date in your phone the day they land.

Mistakes People Make

  • Using the wrong platform. The request starts on the MOFA Visa Platform, not Absher. Absher comes in later, for extensions.
  • Mismatched names. If the spelling on the passport and the birth certificate differ (Mohammad vs Muhammad), fix it before you upload.
  • Letting your own iqama run low. Your visitors' status leans on yours. Check the date with our Iqama Expiry Planner before you apply.
  • Extending too late. Once the permitted stay has passed, an extension is no longer simple. Overstaying brings fines and can block future visas.
  • Forgetting insurance for the extra weeks. An extension needs matching cover.

Check Your Request Status

The MOFA Visa Platform has a "search for a submitted request" option that doesn't need a login. Enter the visitor's passport number, nationality, visa type and port of entry to see whether the request is processing, accepted or rejected. Once your family is in the Kingdom, you can see their visit details on Absher and Muqeem.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Do I apply for a family visit visa through Absher?
    No. The request starts on the MOFA Visa Platform (visa.mofa.gov.sa), where you sign in with Nafath and choose Family Visit Request. Absher is used later, mainly to extend the visit while your family is in Saudi Arabia.
    Who can I invite on a family visit visa?
    Your spouse, your children and your parents, with attested certificates proving the relationship. Siblings and in-laws are usually handled under a separate personal visit request, so choose the request type carefully.
    How much does a family visit visa cost?
    The most widely reported government fee is SAR 300 per visitor, plus small e-service charges and visitor health insurance. Some sources quote higher totals once service charges are added, so rely on the amount the platform shows at checkout.
    How long can my family stay on a visit visa?
    Usually up to 90 days per visit. A multiple-entry visa can be valid for a year, but each stay still has its own limit. The duration printed on the visa is the deadline that applies.
    Can I extend a family visit visa?
    Often, yes. Extensions are requested through Absher while the permitted stay is still running, with an extension fee and insurance that covers the extra period. Do it a week or two early, because it gets much harder once the stay has expired.
    How long does processing take?
    Complete requests are often decided within a few working days. Collecting, translating and attesting documents in your family's home country is usually what takes longest, so start there.
