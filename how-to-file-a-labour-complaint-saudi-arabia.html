How to File a Labour Complaint in Saudi Arabia

Unpaid salary, a final settlement that never arrives, or a contract that isn't being honoured. This is the route that actually gets results, from the first email to the labour court.

Home › Guides › Filing a Labour Complaint

Short answer: you file a labour complaint online through Qiwa (or the HRSD app, or the 19911 hotline). It first goes to an amicable settlement stage, where a ministry officer tries to get you and your employer to agree, usually within about 21 working days. If that fails, the case is referred to the labour court. Filing is free, you don't need a lawyer, and your employer is not allowed to punish you for complaining.

The people who win these cases are usually not the ones with the strongest feelings. They are the ones with the clearest paperwork. Here is how to build that from day one.

Step 1: Raise It in Writing First

Before you file anything, send your employer a short, polite email or letter that says exactly what is wrong and what you want. For example: "My salaries for August and September 2026 (SAR 6,000 each) have not been paid. Please pay them by 15 November."

  • Keep it factual: amounts, months, dates.
  • Send it to HR and your manager, and keep a copy.
  • Give a reasonable deadline, normally one to two weeks.

Many disputes end here. If they don't, this email becomes evidence that you tried to settle it first, which helps you at every later stage.

Step 2: Gather Your Documents

DocumentWhy it matters
Employment contract (from Qiwa)Shows your wage, allowances, contract type and start date
Payslips and bank statementsProve what was paid and what wasn't
Iqama copyIdentifies you and your employer
Emails and WhatsApp messagesShow you raised the issue and what the employer said
Resignation or termination letterSets the end date and who ended the contract

Screenshots are fine, but also export important chats. If you are owed end of service, run the numbers first with our end of service calculator and final settlement calculator so your claim has a clear figure.

Step 3: File the Complaint on Qiwa

  1. Log in to Qiwa with your Absher / Nafath account.
  2. Open the labour dispute (amicable settlement) service and start a new claim.
  3. Choose the claim types that apply, such as unpaid wages, end-of-service award, leave encashment or compensation for unlawful termination. You can claim several in one case.
  4. Enter the amounts for each item and upload your documents.
  5. Submit and note the case number. You will get updates by SMS and in Qiwa.
No Qiwa access? Call the HRSD hotline 19911. Support is available in several languages, and the ministry can also take complaints in person at its offices.

Step 4: The Amicable Settlement Stage

A settlement officer contacts both sides and holds one or more sessions, usually online. The aim is an agreement both of you sign. That agreement is enforceable, so if the employer then fails to pay, you can go straight to enforcement.

  • Attend every session. Missing them can close your case.
  • Bring your calculation of what you are owed.
  • You can accept part of your claim as a compromise, but read the settlement wording carefully before signing, especially any line saying you have no further claims.

If there is no agreement within the settlement period (about 21 working days), the case is referred to the labour court automatically.

Step 5: The Labour Court

Labour courts are part of the Ministry of Justice, and hearings are often held by video. The judge reviews the same documents, so the file you built in steps 1 to 3 is what decides the case. A first judgment commonly takes a few months, and either side can appeal within the deadline the court gives.

Once you have a final judgment, the enforcement court can freeze the employer's accounts and services until you are paid.

Deadlines You Should Not Miss

Don't wait. Claims connected to the employment contract are generally expected to be filed within 12 months of the end of the employment relationship, and the court can refuse claims filed later. If you are still employed, file as soon as it is clear the employer won't fix the problem.

If you plan to leave the country, file before you go on final exit. Following a case from abroad is much harder.

Will My Employer Retaliate?

Filing a complaint is your legal right, and dismissing you for it would be unlawful termination. The bigger practical risk is an employer filing an "absent from work" (huroob) report if you stop going to work. That is why you should keep attending work during the dispute, unless you are leaving under Article 81 (explained in our guide on unpaid salary), and why you should file your complaint before you stop working.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it free to file a labour complaint in Saudi Arabia?
Yes. Filing through Qiwa, the HRSD app or the 19911 hotline is free, and you do not need a lawyer for the settlement stage or the labour court.
How long does a labour complaint take?
The amicable settlement stage usually takes about 21 working days. If it goes to the labour court, a first judgment commonly takes a few months.
Can I file a complaint after leaving Saudi Arabia?
It is possible but much harder to follow. File before you leave on final exit, and keep copies of every document.
Can my employer fire me for filing a complaint?
No. Dismissing a worker for filing a complaint would be unlawful termination, which can entitle you to compensation.
What is the deadline for a labour claim?
Claims are generally expected within 12 months of the end of the employment relationship. File as early as you can.
