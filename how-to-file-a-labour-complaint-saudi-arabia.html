⚖️ How to File a Labour Complaint in Saudi Arabia, and Actually Win

Unpaid salary, a settlement that never arrives, a contract being ignored. This is the route that gets results, from your first email to the labour court.

Nobody takes a job hoping to fight their employer. But when salary stops, a settlement never arrives or a promise gets quietly broken, a formal complaint is often the only thing that makes things move. The good news: it's free, it's online, and you don't need a lawyer.

Short answer: file online through Qiwa (or the HRSD app, or the 19911 hotline). The case first goes to amicable settlement, where a ministry officer tries to get both sides to agree, usually within about 21 working days. If that fails it goes to the labour court. Your employer isn't allowed to punish you for complaining.

The people who win aren't the ones who shout loudest. They're the ones with the clearest paperwork. Here's how to build that from day one.

Step 1: Put It in Writing First

Before filing anything, send your employer a short, polite email that says exactly what's wrong and what you want. For example: "My salaries for August and September 2026 (SAR 6,000 each) have not been paid. Please pay them by 15 November."

  • Stick to facts: amounts, months, dates.
  • Send it to HR and your manager, and keep a copy.
  • Give a reasonable deadline, usually one to two weeks.

Plenty of disputes end right here. If yours doesn't, this email becomes evidence that you tried to settle first, and that helps at every later stage.

Step 2: Gather Your Evidence

DocumentWhy it matters
Employment contract (from Qiwa)Shows your wage, allowances, contract type and start date
Payslips and bank statementsProve what was paid and what wasn't
Iqama copyIdentifies you and your employer
Emails and WhatsApp messagesShow you raised the issue and what the employer said
Resignation or termination letterSets the end date and who ended the contract

Screenshots are fine, but export important chats too. If you're owed end of service, run the numbers with our End of Service Calculator and Final Settlement Calculator so your claim has a clear figure.

Step 3: File on Qiwa

  1. Log in to Qiwa with your Absher / Nafath account.
  2. Open the labour dispute (amicable settlement) service and start a new claim.
  3. Choose every claim type that applies: unpaid wages, end-of-service award, leave encashment, compensation for unlawful termination. You can claim several in one case.
  4. Enter the amount for each item and upload your documents.
  5. Submit and note the case number. Updates come by SMS and in Qiwa.

No Qiwa access? Call 19911, the HRSD hotline, with support in several languages. The ministry also takes complaints in person at its offices.

Step 4: Amicable Settlement

A settlement officer contacts both sides and holds one or more sessions, usually online. The goal is an agreement you both sign, and that agreement is enforceable. If the employer then fails to pay, you can go straight to enforcement.

  • Attend every session. Missing them can close your case.
  • Bring your calculation of exactly what you're owed.
  • Compromise carefully. You can accept part of your claim, but read the wording before signing, especially any line saying you have no further claims.

No agreement within the settlement period (about 21 working days)? The case is referred to the labour court automatically.

Step 5: The Labour Court

Labour courts sit under the Ministry of Justice, and hearings are often held by video. The judge reviews the same documents, so the file you built in steps 1 to 3 is what decides the case. A first judgment commonly takes a few months, and either side can appeal within the deadline the court gives. Once the judgment is final, the enforcement court can freeze the employer's accounts and services until you're paid.

Real Example: Fatima's Final Settlement

Fatima, an accountant in Riyadh, resigned after six years on SAR 10,000. Two months later her settlement still hadn't arrived. Her calculation: SAR 23,333 end of service (two-thirds of a SAR 35,000 award) plus 18 days of unused leave.

She emailed HR, waited ten days, then filed on Qiwa with her contract, resignation letter and calculation attached. At the second settlement session the company agreed to pay in full within 14 days and signed the agreement. When it paid three days late, she didn't need court at all. The signed settlement was already enforceable.

Deadlines You Must Not Miss

  • 12 months: claims connected to the employment contract are generally expected within 12 months of the job ending, and later claims can be refused.
  • Before final exit: file before you leave the country. Following a case from abroad is much harder.
  • Still employed? File as soon as it's clear the employer won't fix the problem.

Will My Employer Retaliate?

Filing is your legal right, and dismissing you for it would be unlawful termination. The bigger practical risk is an employer filing an "absent from work" (huroob) report if you stop turning up. So keep attending work during the dispute, unless you're leaving under Article 81 (see our unpaid salary guide), and always file before you stop working.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it free to file a labour complaint in Saudi Arabia?
Yes. Filing through Qiwa, the HRSD app or the 19911 hotline is free, and you don't need a lawyer for the settlement stage or the labour court.
How long does a labour complaint take?
The amicable settlement stage usually takes about 21 working days. If it goes to the labour court, a first judgment commonly takes a few months.
Can I file a complaint after leaving Saudi Arabia?
It's possible but much harder to follow. File before you leave on final exit, and keep copies of everything.
Can my employer fire me for filing a complaint?
No. Dismissing a worker for filing a complaint would be unlawful termination, which can entitle you to compensation.
What is the deadline for a labour claim?
Claims are generally expected within 12 months of the end of the employment relationship. File as early as you can.
What happens if my employer ignores the settlement agreement?
A signed settlement is enforceable, so you can go to enforcement without starting a new court case.
