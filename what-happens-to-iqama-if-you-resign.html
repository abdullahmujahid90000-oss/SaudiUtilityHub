🪪 Resigning in Saudi Arabia? Here's Exactly What Happens to Your Iqama

The 60-day window, huroob, transfers and final exit, explained the way we wish someone had explained them to us before we pressed "send".

⏳ The 60-day rule🔄 Qiwa transfers🚫 Avoiding huroob
Home › Iqama After Resignation

Your resignation letter is written. Your finger is hovering over "send". And one question is keeping you up at night: what happens to my iqama? Will you be stuck? Reported as huroob? Forced to leave next week?

Breathe. Resigning the right way doesn't put you at risk. It starts a clock, and once you know how long that clock runs, you're in control.

Short answer: resigning doesn't cancel your iqama. It stays valid while you serve your notice. Once the contract ends you get 60 days to either move to a new employer through Qiwa or leave on a final exit visa. Do neither and your employer can report you as absent from work, which is what everyone calls "huroob" and is the one status you really want to avoid.

Much of what gets forwarded in WhatsApp groups is out of date, because the rules changed in 2021 and again in 2025. Here's how it works today.

Step 1: Hand In Your Resignation the Right Way

Put it in writing. An email to HR is fine, but keep a copy and note the date. Under the Labour Law amendments in force since February 2025:

  • The employer has 30 days to respond. Silence for 30 days counts as acceptance.
  • They can postpone it once, by up to 60 days, but only for a genuine work reason given to you in writing within those 30 days.
  • You can withdraw it within 7 days, as long as it hasn't been accepted yet.
  • Notice on an open-ended contract is at least 30 days from you, unless your contract says longer. See our notice period guide.

During your notice nothing changes with your iqama. You're still employed, still paid, still on GOSI, and still building service towards end of service.

Fixed-term contract? Leaving before the end date can mean compensating the employer for the rest of the term, unless your contract says otherwise. Read the termination clause first.

Step 2: The Contract Ends and Your 60 Days Begin

Once your last working day passes, your contract shows as ended in Qiwa, and the 60-day window opens. You have three doors:

OptionWhat it meansWho starts it
Transfer to a new employerYour iqama moves to the new company. Since the 2021 Labour Reform Initiative you don't need your old employer's permission once the contract has ended.The new employer, through Qiwa
Final exitYou leave Saudi Arabia for good and your iqama is cancelled.The employer, or you without their approval once the contract has ended
Stay with the same employerYou agree a new contract and carry on.You and the employer

Sixty days disappears faster than you'd think, especially while a new offer is still being negotiated. If you plan to transfer, get the new job lined up before your last day.

Step 3: The Huroob Trap, and How to Stay Out of It

If the 60 days pass with no transfer, no final exit and no new contract, you can be reported absent from work (انقطاع عن العمل) in Qiwa. That's huroob. An employer can also file it earlier, while your contract is still running, if you simply stop turning up. That's why walking away without a written resignation is so dangerous.

Being marked absent hurts:

  • Normal transfers and final exit requests can stop going through.
  • You can't legally work for anyone else.
  • Your iqama can't be renewed and your work permit lapses.
  • You face fines and possibly deportation, which can make a future Saudi visa difficult.

Can it be fixed? Sometimes. If the report is recent, a transfer or final exit may still be possible within 60 days of the report date. Qiwa has also run correction programmes for workers who've been in the Kingdom for more than 12 months, where a new employer takes you on and pays any outstanding fees. Check the current options in Qiwa yourself, and steer clear of anyone charging money to "remove huroob". If your employer reported you falsely, keep every message and payslip and file a labour complaint.

Real Example: Ravi Gets It Right

Ravi, a site engineer in Dammam on SAR 9,000, gets a better offer in Riyadh. He emails his resignation on 1 March and HR accepts it on 10 March. His 30-day notice ends on 31 March.

  • Before his last day he signs the new offer, and the new company prepares the transfer in Qiwa.
  • Early April, within days of his contract ending, the transfer is submitted. His old employer's approval isn't needed.
  • By mid-April his old employer pays his final settlement, within the two weeks the law allows after a resignation.

He had 4 years 11 months of service. Waiting one more month to pass five years would have doubled his resignation payout, because the share jumps from one-third to two-thirds. That's worth checking before you send any letter.

Your End of Service When You Resign

Service with the employerWhat you get if you resign
Less than 2 yearsNothing
2 to 5 yearsOne-third of the full award
5 to 10 yearsTwo-thirds
10 years or moreThe full award

The full award is half a month's wage for each of the first five years and a month's wage for each year after that (Article 84). After a resignation, final dues must be paid within two weeks of the contract ending. Leaving on Final Exit, and Coming Back Later

Leave properly on a final exit and there's no ban on returning. Any Saudi employer can bring you back on a new work visa, though you'll go through the full process again (medical, stamping, new iqama). Before you fly:

  • Pay traffic fines and any outstanding dependent fee, or the final exit can't be issued.
  • Your dependants need their own final exit visas and should leave before you or with you.
  • Settle loans and credit cards, because unpaid debt can lead to a travel ban through the courts.
  • Book your flight once the visa is issued. A final exit not used in time can lapse with a fine.

Before You Press Send: Checklist

  • Check your contract type (open-ended or fixed-term) and its notice period.
  • Check your service length. Are you close to the 2-, 5- or 10-year mark?
  • Resign in writing and keep proof of the date.
  • Staying in Saudi Arabia? Get the new offer in writing before your last day.
  • Check your iqama expiry date. If it runs out within the 60 days, ask how renewal will be handled.
  • Leaving? Clear fines and dependent fees early.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a grace period after I resign in Saudi Arabia?
Yes. Once your contract ends you have 60 days to transfer to a new employer through Qiwa, leave on final exit, or sign a new contract with the same employer.
When does huroob happen after resignation?
If the 60 days after your contract ends pass without a transfer or exit, you can be reported absent from work, which is what people call huroob. An employer can also report it earlier if you stop coming to work while the contract is still running.
Can I transfer my iqama without my employer's permission after resigning?
Yes, once your contract has ended. The new employer submits the transfer through Qiwa, and your old employer's approval isn't needed.
Can I come back to Saudi Arabia after final exit?
Yes. A normal final exit carries no ban. You can return on a new work visa from any employer.
Does my iqama get cancelled the day I resign?
No. It stays valid through your notice and the 60-day window after the contract ends, as long as it hasn't expired.
How long does my employer have to accept my resignation?
30 days. Silence counts as acceptance. They can postpone it by up to 60 days only for a genuine work reason, given to you in writing within the first 30 days.
