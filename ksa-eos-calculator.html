Estimator based on Labour Law Articles 84 & 85 · ½ month/yr (first 5 yrs) + 1 month/yr (after) · Resignation reduced per Article 85
Under Saudi Labour Law Article 84, End of Service Gratuity is a half-month wage for each of the first 5 years of service plus a full-month wage for each year beyond 5, based on the worker's wage. If you resign, Article 85 reduces the award to one-third (2–5 years), two-thirds (5–10 years), or the full amount (10+ years). Termination by the employer, contract expiry, or retirement pays the full award. [1]

📖 How to Use This EOS Estimator

This free tool helps employees in Saudi Arabia estimate their end-of-service entitlement under Article 84 of the Labour Law. Whether you're resigning, being terminated, or reaching the end of a fixed-term contract, it computes an estimated gratuity and shows the full calculation breakdown so you can check each step.

  1. Enter Your Wage
    Input the monthly wage figure used for your pay, in SAR. See "What counts as wage" in the FAQ if you are unsure whether to include a housing or transport allowance.
  2. Select Contract Type
    Choose between unlimited or fixed-term contract. Both follow the same Article 84 rules for the EOS calculation itself.
  3. Choose Reason for Leaving
    Select resignation, or termination/contract expiry/retirement. This determines the Article 85 reduction applied to your award.
  4. Enter Service Duration
    Input your total years and additional months (0–11) of continuous service with this employer.
  5. Calculate EOS
    See an estimated gratuity amount with a full breakdown, based on Saudi Labour Law Article 84.
What this tool does not cover: it does not model Article 80 serious-misconduct dismissals, the female-worker marriage/childbirth full-award exception, the force-majeure full-award exception, GOSI pension interactions, or domestic workers (who follow a separate formula). The FAQ below explains how each of these works.

📜 Basic Salary or Full Wage? What Counts

A lot of people assume end of service is worked out on basic salary only. That is not quite what the law says, and it is the most common reason two calculations of the same settlement come out different.

  • HRSD's own official EOS calculator asks for your "actual wage", not your basic salary [2].
  • HRSD's guidance defines "wage" as the basic wage plus the other amounts due to you under the contract. Only commissions, percentages and similar pay that naturally goes up and down can be left out, and only if you and the employer agreed to that [1].
  • So a fixed monthly housing or transport allowance is normally part of the wage used for end of service. Variable pay may not be.

If your employer offers a settlement based on basic salary alone, ask them to show which clause of your contract excludes the allowances. For a cautious figure, run the calculator twice: once with basic salary and once with basic plus fixed allowances. Your real entitlement is usually closer to the second number.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the EOS formula in Saudi Arabia?
Under Article 84 of Saudi Labour Law, the end-of-service award is a half-month wage for each of the first 5 years of service plus a full-month wage for each year beyond 5, based on the worker's wage. On resignation, Article 85 reduces this to one-third of the award for 2 to 5 years of service, two-thirds for more than 5 but under 10 years, and the full award at 10+ years. Termination by the employer, contract expiry, or retirement pays the full award.
Do I get EOS if I resign?
Yes, if you have at least 2 years of service. Under Article 85, resignation pays one-third of the full Article 84 award for 2 to 5 years of service, two-thirds for more than 5 but under 10 years, and the full award at 10 years or more. Under 2 years of service, resignation pays no award.
What if my employer terminates me?
In most cases you receive the full Article 84 award: a half-month wage for each of the first 5 years plus a full-month wage for each year beyond 5, regardless of service duration. An exception applies if the employer ends the contract for the specific serious-misconduct grounds listed in Article 80 — see our Article 80 answer below, which this calculator does not model.
What counts as "wage" for the EOS calculation?
Saudi Labour Law bases the end-of-service award on the worker's "wage," which HRSD defines as the basic wage plus other amounts due under the work contract. HRSD's own official EOS calculator asks for your "actual wage," and HRSD's published guidance says only commissions, percentages, and similar components that genuinely vary may be excluded from this base, and only if employer and worker have agreed to exclude them. In practice, a fixed, regular contractual allowance (for example, a standing housing or transport allowance) is generally expected to be included, while pay that truly fluctuates may not be. If your contract is unclear, check its exact wording with HR.
Is there a minimum service period for EOS?
For resignation: a minimum of 2 years is required before any award is due. For termination by the employer, contract expiry, or retirement: there is no minimum period — the award (however small) is due from the first day of service, prorated for partial years.
Does EOS apply to expatriates?
Yes, the Article 84 end-of-service award applies to private-sector employees in Saudi Arabia generally, including expatriate workers. Domestic workers are covered by a separate set of regulations with a different formula, not this one.
How is partial year calculated?
Partial years are prorated in proportion to time actually worked. For example, 2 years and 6 months of service is treated as 2.5 years when applying the Article 84 formula.
Can I transfer my EOS to a new employer?
No. The end-of-service award is paid out by your current employer when your employment with them ends. It resets with a new employer and cannot be carried over or transferred.
Does a fixed-term contract get the same EOS as an unlimited contract?
Yes. HRSD guidance confirms the end-of-service award is due at the end of the employment relationship whether the contract is fixed-term or open-ended (unlimited). When a fixed-term contract simply completes its agreed term, this is treated as an employer-side ending for EOS purposes (full Article 84 award) rather than as a resignation.
What if I'm dismissed for serious misconduct (Article 80)?
Article 80 of the Labour Law lists specific serious-misconduct grounds — such as assault, breach of trust or integrity, disclosing trade secrets, or extended unauthorized absence — under which an employer may end a contract without notice, after giving the worker a chance to respond. In those cases Article 80 lets the employer end the contract without notice, compensation or end-of-service award, so this calculator does not apply. If you think Article 80 was used against you unfairly, you can file a claim through Qiwa's labour dispute service.
How soon must my employer pay my EOS?
Article 88 of the Labour Law sets the deadline. If the employer ends the contract, all dues must be paid within one week. If you resign, the employer has up to two weeks. Our final settlement calculator adds up everything else you are owed.
How much end of service benefit do I get after 2 years in Saudi Arabia?
For the first five years of service the Labour Law formula gives half a month's wage per year, so two completed years is roughly one month's wage in total. On a SAR 8,000 wage that is about SAR 8,000 if the contract ends by termination. If you resigned after exactly two years, Article 85 cuts that to one-third, so about SAR 2,667. Resign before you complete two years and nothing is due.
Is end of service calculated on basic salary or gross salary?
The award is based on the last wage, and "wage" under the Labour Law means basic pay plus the other amounts due under your contract, such as a fixed housing or transport allowance. Commissions and similar pay that genuinely varies can be left out only if you and the employer agreed to it. Before you sign the settlement, ask HR which wage base they used.
Do unpaid leave periods count towards end of service?
Short unpaid leave makes no difference. Under Article 116 of the Labour Law, unpaid leave longer than 20 days suspends your service for that period unless your contract says otherwise. If you took a long unpaid break, subtract it from your service before using the calculator.
Can I estimate my end of service if my salary changed during employment?
Yes — the award is calculated on the last wage, not on an average of past salaries, so enter your current or final wage even if you earned less earlier. If much of your pay is commission or piece-rate, employers usually average it over a recent period, so ask HR which months they used.
