⚖️ End of Service Calculator: See What You're Really Owed

Years of work come down to one number on your final settlement. Check it here in seconds, before you resign, before you sign, and before anyone rounds it down.

Labour Law Articles 84 & 85 · ½ month a year for the first 5 years, 1 month a year after · Updated November 2026
The short version: Saudi Labour Law gives you half a month's wage for each of your first 5 years, then a full month's wage for every year after that. If your employer ends the contract, or a fixed-term contract simply runs out, you get 100%. If you resign, you get nothing under 2 years, one-third from 2 to 5 years, two-thirds from 5 to 10 years, and the full amount after 10. [1]

Estimate Your End of Service Gratuity

Enter the monthly wage figure your employment contract uses to determine pay. Saudi Labour Law bases the award on the worker's "wage," and HRSD's own official calculator asks for "actual wage." Only variable pay (commissions, percentages, and similar amounts that genuinely fluctuate) may be excluded, and only by agreement — see "What counts as wage" below. If you are unsure which figure applies to you, check your contract or ask HR; you can also enter your basic salary alone for a conservative, lower-bound estimate. 📖 How the Number Is Worked Out

Two things decide your award: how long you've worked for this employer, and how the job ended. Enter your last monthly wage, your years and months of service and the reason for leaving above, and the calculator does the rest, showing every step so you can check it against your employer's figure.

📊 Four Real-Life Examples

Ali resigns after 3½ years

Ali, a sales coordinator in Riyadh on SAR 10,000, takes a better offer after 3 years 6 months. Full award: 3.5 × ½ month = 1.75 months = SAR 17,500. As a resignation between 2 and 5 years he gets one-third: SAR 5,833.

Fatima's contract is ended after 2 years

Fatima's employer in Jeddah ends her contract after exactly 2 years on SAR 8,000. Because the employer ended it, there's no reduction: 2 × ½ month = 1 month = SAR 8,000. Had she resigned on the same day, she'd have received one-third: about SAR 2,667.

Omar resigns after 8 years

Omar, a site engineer on SAR 12,000, resigns after 8 years. First 5 years = 2.5 months, next 3 years = 3 months, so 5.5 months = SAR 66,000. Resigning between 5 and 10 years gives two-thirds: SAR 44,000.

Huda's fixed-term contract simply ends after 12 years

Huda's contract in Dammam reaches its end date and isn't renewed. That's not a resignation, so she gets the full award: 2.5 + 7 = 9.5 months × SAR 15,000 = SAR 142,500.

💡 Basic Salary or Full Wage? The Question Worth Thousands

Many employers quietly calculate end of service on basic salary only. That's often not what the law says, and it's the most common reason two calculations of the same settlement don't match.

  • HRSD's own end-of-service calculator asks for your "actual wage", not your basic salary [2].
  • HRSD defines the wage as basic pay plus the other amounts due under your contract. Only pay that genuinely goes up and down, such as commissions, can be left out, and only if you agreed to that [1].
  • So a fixed monthly housing or transport allowance normally counts.

On a SAR 6,000 basic with a SAR 1,500 housing allowance, 6 years of service is worth SAR 21,000 on basic alone, or SAR 26,250 on the full wage. That SAR 5,250 gap is why it's worth asking HR, in writing, which wage they used.

🚫 Mistakes That Cost People Their Gratuity

  • Resigning when your contract was about to end. If a fixed-term contract is a few weeks from its end date, let it expire instead. Expiry pays 100%; resignation may pay a third.
  • Signing a settlement on the spot. Take it home, run the numbers here, and check the wage base before you sign anything that says "no further claims".
  • Forgetting long unpaid leave. Unpaid leave of more than 20 days isn't counted as service unless your contract says otherwise, so subtract it before you calculate.
  • Confusing end of service with your final settlement. Unused annual leave, your last salary and any notice pay are paid on top. Add them up with the final settlement calculator.
  • Waiting too long when the money doesn't arrive. Your employer must pay within one week if they ended the contract, or two weeks if you resigned. After that, file a claim on Qiwa.
Not covered by this calculator: dismissal for serious misconduct under Article 80, the special full-award cases for working women after marriage or childbirth, force majeure, and domestic workers, who follow their own rules.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the end of service formula in Saudi Arabia?
Half a month's wage for each of the first 5 years of service, then one month's wage for each year after that, based on your last wage. Partial years count proportionally.
Do I get end of service if I resign?
Yes, if you have at least 2 years of service. You get one-third of the full award for 2 to 5 years, two-thirds for 5 to 10 years, and the full award after 10 years. Under 2 years, resignation pays nothing.
What if my employer ends my contract?
You get the full award with no reduction, from your first year of service. The exception is dismissal for serious misconduct under Article 80.
Is end of service calculated on basic salary or the full wage?
On your wage, which HRSD defines as basic pay plus the other amounts due under your contract, such as a fixed housing or transport allowance. Only genuinely variable pay like commissions can be excluded, and only by agreement.
How much end of service do I get after 5 years?
2.5 months of your wage if the contract ends or you're terminated. On SAR 10,000 that's SAR 25,000. If you resign at exactly 5 years, you get two-thirds, about SAR 16,667.
Does a fixed-term contract that ends count as resignation?
No. When a fixed-term contract simply reaches its end date and isn't renewed, you get the full award, even if you had less than 2 years.
How soon must my employer pay end of service?
Within one week if the employer ended the contract, and within two weeks if you resigned.
Do unpaid leave periods count towards end of service?
Unpaid leave of more than 20 days isn't counted as service unless your contract says otherwise. Short unpaid leave makes no difference.
