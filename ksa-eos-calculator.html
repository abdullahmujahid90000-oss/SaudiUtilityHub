Estimator based on Labour Law Articles 84 & 85 · ½ month/yr (first 5 yrs) + 1 month/yr (after) · Resignation reduced per Article 85
Under Saudi Labour Law Article 84, End of Service Gratuity is a half-month wage for each of the first 5 years of service plus a full-month wage for each year beyond 5, based on the worker's wage. If you resign, Article 85 reduces the award to one-third (2–5 years), two-thirds (5–10 years), or the full amount (10+ years). Termination by the employer, contract expiry, or retirement pays the full award. [1]

📜 How the Wage Base Was Resolved

Earlier versions of pages like this one often state flatly that "only basic salary counts" for EOS. We could not confirm that as the current, complete legal position, so we researched the primary sources directly rather than repeating it. Here is what we found and how we handled it:

  • HRSD's own official EOS calculator requires an input labeled "Actual wage" — not "basic salary" [2].
  • HRSD's official guidance on the end-of-service award states that "wage" means the basic wage plus other amounts due to the worker under the contract, and that only commissions, percentages, and similar components which are by their nature subject to increase or decrease may be excluded from the calculation base — and only if employer and worker have specifically agreed to exclude them [1].
  • Read together, this indicates the wage base is broader than "basic salary only": a fixed, regular contractual allowance is not the kind of fluctuating component the exclusion is meant for, so it is generally expected to be included by default, while genuinely variable pay may be excluded by agreement.

We are not a licensed Saudi law firm, and a specific allowance in a specific contract can still be a fact-specific question (for example, whether a "transport allowance" in your contract is a fixed monthly sum or a variable reimbursement changes the answer). If your case is not a straightforward fixed monthly allowance, confirm directly with HRSD, Qiwa, or a qualified employment lawyer rather than relying solely on this estimate.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the EOS formula in Saudi Arabia?
Under Article 84 of Saudi Labour Law, the end-of-service award is a half-month wage for each of the first 5 years of service plus a full-month wage for each year beyond 5, based on the worker's wage. On resignation, Article 85 reduces this to one-third of the award for 2 to 5 years of service, two-thirds for more than 5 but under 10 years, and the full award at 10+ years. Termination by the employer, contract expiry, or retirement pays the full award.
Do I get EOS if I resign?
Yes, if you have at least 2 years of service. Under Article 85, resignation pays one-third of the full Article 84 award for 2 to 5 years of service, two-thirds for more than 5 but under 10 years, and the full award at 10 years or more. Under 2 years of service, resignation pays no award.
What if my employer terminates me?
In most cases you receive the full Article 84 award: a half-month wage for each of the first 5 years plus a full-month wage for each year beyond 5, regardless of service duration. An exception applies if the employer ends the contract for the specific serious-misconduct grounds listed in Article 80 — see our Article 80 answer below, which this calculator does not model.
What counts as "wage" for the EOS calculation?
Saudi Labour Law bases the end-of-service award on the worker's "wage," which HRSD defines as the basic wage plus other amounts due under the work contract. HRSD's own official EOS calculator asks for your "actual wage," and HRSD's published guidance says only commissions, percentages, and similar components that genuinely vary may be excluded from this base, and only if employer and worker have agreed to exclude them. This suggests a fixed, regular contractual allowance (for example, a standing housing or transport allowance) is generally expected to be included, while pay that truly fluctuates may not be. This is a fact-specific question about your own contract, so check its exact wording and confirm with HRSD, Qiwa, or a labor lawyer if you are unsure.
Is there a minimum service period for EOS?
For resignation: a minimum of 2 years is required before any award is due. For termination by the employer, contract expiry, or retirement: there is no minimum period — the award (however small) is due from the first day of service, prorated for partial years.
Does EOS apply to expatriates?
Yes, the Article 84 end-of-service award applies to private-sector employees in Saudi Arabia generally, including expatriate workers. Domestic workers are covered by a separate set of regulations with a different formula, not this one.
How is partial year calculated?
Partial years are prorated in proportion to time actually worked. For example, 2 years and 6 months of service is treated as 2.5 years when applying the Article 84 formula.
Can I transfer my EOS to a new employer?
No. The end-of-service award is paid out by your current employer when your employment with them ends. It resets with a new employer and cannot be carried over or transferred.
Does a fixed-term contract get the same EOS as an unlimited contract?
Yes. HRSD guidance confirms the end-of-service award is due at the end of the employment relationship whether the contract is fixed-term or open-ended (unlimited). When a fixed-term contract simply completes its agreed term, this is treated as an employer-side ending for EOS purposes (full Article 84 award) rather than as a resignation.
What if I'm dismissed for serious misconduct (Article 80)?
Article 80 of the Labour Law lists specific serious-misconduct grounds — such as assault, breach of trust or integrity, disclosing trade secrets, or extended unauthorized absence — under which an employer may end a contract without notice, after giving the worker a chance to respond. Whether and how this affects the end-of-service award in a given case is a fact-specific legal question with sources we found giving inconsistent detail on the exact thresholds involved. This calculator does not model Article 80 outcomes. If you believe Article 80 has been invoked in your case, verify your entitlement with HRSD or a qualified Saudi employment lawyer before relying on any online estimate.
How soon must my employer pay my EOS?
Saudi Labour Law requires final dues, including the end-of-service award, to be settled promptly after the contract ends. We found different specific deadlines cited across sources and could not confirm one single statutory number from a primary HRSD source, so confirm the applicable deadline for your case with HRSD, the Qiwa portal, or your employer's HR department.
