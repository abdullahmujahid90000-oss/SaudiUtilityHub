🔄 Moving to a New Employer? Your Iqama Transfer, Step by Step

Whether your contract has ended, your salary has stopped, or you've just found something better: how Qiwa transfers work, and when your current boss can't stop you.

🔓 When approval isn't needed 💰 Who pays the fee Updated November 2026

The new job offer is real, the salary is better, and you're ready to move. Then the worry kicks in: Will my company let me go? Who pays? What if something goes wrong in between?

Short answer: your new employer starts the transfer through Qiwa and normally pays the fee. Once your contract has ended, or if your salary has gone unpaid for 3 months, you usually don't need your current employer's approval. Use the checklist below to make sure everything is in place before you resign.

    🔎 How an Iqama Transfer Actually Happens

    Since the 2021 labour reforms, moving to a new employer runs through Qiwa. The important thing to know is that the new employer starts it, not you.

    1. The new employer sends a job offer in Qiwa. It shows the salary and contract terms.
    2. You accept it in your Qiwa account.
    3. The new employer submits the transfer request. If your current contract is still running, your current employer is notified.
    4. The fee is paid, normally by the new employer, and the transfer completes. Your iqama now shows the new sponsor.

    A Qiwa job offer on its own isn't a transfer. Until step 4 is done, you're still employed by your current employer, so keep working.

    🔓 When You Don't Need Your Current Employer's Approval

    This is the part most expats don't realise. You can usually move without your current employer's consent when:

    • ✅ Your contract has ended (expired, or your resignation and notice period are complete).
    • ✅ Your salary hasn't been paid for 3 consecutive months.
    • ✅ Your employer let your iqama or work permit expire and didn't renew it.
    • ✅ You were falsely reported as absent from work (huroob).
    • ✅ Your employer is in the Red Nitaqat band.

    Qiwa checks these conditions when the new employer submits the request. If you're stuck because your salary isn't being paid, read what to do when your salary isn't paid.

    💰 Who Pays the Transfer Fee? (Not You)

    Article 40 of the Labour Law puts the cost of a service transfer, along with iqama renewal, profession change and exit re-entry fees, on the employer. For a transfer, that's normally the new employer.

    Transfer numberFee commonly reported
    1stSAR 2,000
    2ndSAR 4,000
    3rd and laterSAR 6,000

    These are the figures reported across legal and expat sources. The exact amount for your case appears on the Qiwa or SADAD payment screen. Enter that figure in the worksheet above. If a recruiter or new employer asks you to pay it, ask them to put that in writing and check Article 40 with them.

    📐 Three Situations We See All the Time

    Sameer: a better offer, contract finished

    His 2-year contract ended last month. The new company sends a Qiwa offer, he accepts, they pay the SAR 2,000 first-transfer fee, and he moves without needing his old employer's approval.

    Joy: salary unpaid since July

    Three months unpaid. She files a complaint on Qiwa first to create a record, then her new employer submits the transfer. The unpaid-salary condition means her current employer's consent isn't needed.

    Faisal: not sure the new company is allowed to hire

    He asks the new employer to confirm their Nitaqat status in Qiwa before he resigns, so he isn't left between jobs if the transfer is blocked.

    🚫 Transfer Mistakes That Leave People Stuck

    • Resigning before the offer is in Qiwa. Get the offer registered first. A verbal promise doesn't protect you.
    • Letting the 60-day window run out. After your contract ends, you have about 60 days to transfer or leave. See what happens to your iqama if you resign.
    • Agreeing to pay the transfer fee yourself. It's the employer's cost under Article 40.
    • Walking out of your current job. That can lead to an absent-from-work report. Serve your notice, or file a complaint first if you're leaving because of a breach.
    • Ignoring an expiring passport. Some services are blocked when the passport is close to expiry, so sort it out early.

    ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

    How do I transfer my iqama to a new employer?
    The new employer sends you a job offer in Qiwa. You accept it, the new employer submits the transfer request, and once the fee is paid the transfer completes.
    Can I transfer my iqama without my employer's approval?
    Yes, in several cases: when your contract has ended, when your salary hasn't been paid for 3 consecutive months, when your employer let your iqama expire, after a false absconding report, or when the employer is in the Red Nitaqat band.
    Who pays the iqama transfer fee?
    The employer. Article 40 of the Labour Law puts transfer fees on the employer, normally the new employer for a transfer.
    How much is the iqama transfer fee?
    It's commonly reported as SAR 2,000 for the first transfer, SAR 4,000 for the second and SAR 6,000 for the third and later. The exact figure appears on the Qiwa or SADAD payment screen.
    How long do I have to transfer after my contract ends?
    About 60 days to transfer to a new employer or leave on final exit, before you can be reported as absent from work.
    Do I need to have worked a minimum time before transferring?
    Sources commonly mention around 12 months with your first employer before an ordinary transfer, unless one of the exception conditions applies. Qiwa checks this when the request is submitted.
