    ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

    What does this tool actually calculate?
    It takes the official Iqama expiry date you type in and a check date (defaulting to today), then calculates how many days remain or have passed, plus optional reminder dates (30/60/90 days before expiry, or a custom lead time). It does not look up, verify, or confirm your expiry date against any government record — it only does arithmetic on the date you provide.
    Where do I find my official Iqama expiry date?
    Check Absher (absher.sa) under individual services, or Muqeem/Absher Business if your employer manages your record. These portals typically display the expiry date in both Gregorian and Hijri calendars. Enter the Gregorian date shown in your official record into this planner — we do not convert between calendars ourselves.
    Does this tool check Absher, Muqeem, or Jawazat for me?
    No. This planner has no connection to any government system and cannot see your actual Iqama record. It only calculates dates from what you type in. For your real, current status, always check Absher, Muqeem, or your employer directly.
    How much does Iqama renewal cost, and is there a grace period or late penalty?
    We found inconsistent figures across independent sources for renewal fees, grace periods, and late penalties, and no single official fee schedule we could confidently cite as current and universal — these details vary by Iqama category (work, dependant, domestic worker) and can change. This planner does not calculate or display any fee or penalty amount. Confirm current figures on Absher, Muqeem, or with your employer/sponsor before budgeting or making decisions.
    How early can I renew before my Iqama expires?
    Sources we reviewed disagreed on the exact renewal window, so we do not state a specific number of days as fact. Use the reminder feature in this planner to give yourself lead time, then confirm the exact renewal-eligibility window on Absher or Muqeem when you're ready to act.
    What happens if my Iqama expires before I renew?
    We cannot tell you, and this planner does not attempt to. It will only tell you that the date you entered has passed and by how many days. Some sources describe consequences such as fines for late renewal; a specific claim about automatic deportation after a fixed number of days could not be confirmed against a primary source in our review and has been removed rather than repeated. Contact your employer/sponsor or check Absher/Muqeem directly if your Iqama has expired.
    Do I need a medical test to renew my Iqama?
    Valid health insurance and a clean record of resolved fines are commonly listed as general conditions for government transactions in Saudi Arabia, including Iqama renewal. We did not find confirmation that a specific medical test is a universal requirement for every Iqama renewal — this appears to vary by category. Verify the exact requirements for your case on Absher or Muqeem.
    Is my Iqama number or personal data stored or sent anywhere?
    This tool never asks for your Iqama number, name, or passport number, and it never should — we do not need it to calculate dates. All calculations run in your browser; the dates you enter are not sent to any server or stored anywhere by this tool.
    Who is responsible for renewing my Iqama — me or my employer?
    For work-visa holders, renewal is often initiated by the employer/sponsor through Muqeem or Absher Business, though individuals can view their own status through Absher. Responsibility can vary by employer and case type, so confirm directly with your employer or sponsor who is expected to act.

    🔗 Official Verification & Renewal Links

    • absher.sa — Absher (Ministry of Interior), the official individuals' portal for checking Iqama status and starting renewal.
    • muqeem.sa — Muqeem, typically used by employers/sponsors to manage expatriate worker and dependant residency records.
    • my.gov.sa — Renewal of Iqama (residence permit) — the National Platform's official service page describing the renewal service.
    • moi.gov.sa — Ministry of Interior, overseeing General Directorate of Passports (Jawazat) matters.
