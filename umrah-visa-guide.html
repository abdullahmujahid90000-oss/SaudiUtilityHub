🕋 Umrah Made Simple: Your Visa, Your Nusuk Permit, Your Journey

Two things stand between you and the Haram: getting into the country, and getting a permit slot. Here's how to sort both, whether you live in Riyadh or are flying in from abroad.

📱 Nusuk permit🛂 eVisa & on arrival🪪 Iqama holders
Home › Umrah Visa Guide

Maybe you're a resident who has been meaning to go "one of these weekends" for a year. Maybe your parents are visiting and this is their chance. Either way, Umrah has never been easier to arrange, as long as you get two separate things right: the visa that lets you into the country, and the Nusuk permit that lets you into the Haram.

Short answer: most people don't need a special Umrah visa any more. Iqama holders just book a free Umrah permit in the Nusuk app. Visitors can perform Umrah on a tourist eVisa (one year, multiple entry, up to 90 days per visit), a visa on arrival, or a family or visit visa, and they book the same Nusuk permit. The main exception is the Hajj season, when Umrah pauses for several weeks.

Which Route Fits You?

Booking Your Nusuk Permit, Step by Step

  1. Download Nusuk (iOS or Android) and register with your iqama or passport details.
  2. Choose Umrah, then pick a date and time slot. Popular slots, especially Ramadan nights and Fridays, go quickly.
  3. Confirm. The permit appears with a QR code, and it's free.
  4. Book Rawdah separately if you want to pray in the Rawdah at the Prophet's Mosque in Madinah. It has its own permit and limited slots.
  5. Show the QR code when asked on the way into the Haram, and keep a screenshot in case the signal drops.

Coming From Abroad: The Visa Part

  1. Check eligibility on visa.mofa.gov.sa. If your nationality isn't on the eVisa list, use a licensed Umrah operator.
  2. Prepare: a passport valid for at least six months, a photo, accommodation details and a return ticket. Medical insurance is bundled into the eVisa fee.
  3. Apply and pay by card. Approval often comes within minutes to a few days.
  4. Book Nusuk as soon as your visa is issued, then travel with digital and printed copies of both.

Sources quote the tourist eVisa at anything from about SAR 395 to SAR 535 including insurance, so the price at checkout is the one to go by.

Example: Bilal's Weekend Umrah From Riyadh

Bilal works in Riyadh and finally decides to go. On Monday he books a Thursday-night Nusuk slot and a Saturday Rawdah slot. On Thursday he flies to Jeddah, having changed into ihram before boarding, and makes his intention before the plane passes the miqat (for travellers from Riyadh that's Qarn al-Manazil). He takes a taxi to Makkah and completes Umrah after Isha. On Friday he takes the Haramain train to Madinah, which is about two hours, prays in the Rawdah on Saturday and flies home that evening.

His total was a little over SAR 2,000 for flights, a mid-range hotel and the train. The permits cost nothing. Booking a few weeks ahead outside Ramadan is what kept it cheap.

What It Costs

  • Nusuk permit: free
  • Tourist eVisa (visitors): about SAR 395–535 including insurance
  • Flights: Jeddah (JED) is closest to Makkah, and Madinah (MED) suits a Madinah-first trip
  • Hotels: prices near the Haram can triple in Ramadan and school holidays. Aziziyah and Misfalah are cheaper, with shuttle buses.
  • Haramain high-speed train: links Jeddah, Makkah and Madinah

For a quieter, cheaper Umrah, go in the months right after the Hajj season (Muharram and Safar). The last ten nights of Ramadan are the most rewarding and the most crowded.

Mistakes People Make

  • Turning up without a Nusuk permit. A visa or iqama alone isn't enough for the Haram.
  • Forgetting the Hajj season. Umrah pauses for several weeks around Hajj, and the exact dates are announced each year. One 2026 notice, for example, said normal Umrah services would resume on 31 May.
  • Buying "packages" on social media. Use licensed operators only. Unlicensed Umrah deals are a common visa scam.
  • Passing the miqat without ihram. If you're flying, change at home or at the airport, and make your intention before the announced point.
  • Skipping vaccinations. Proof of meningitis (ACWY) vaccination may be requested, and the Ministry of Health publishes the current list each season.

Good to Know

  • Women can perform Umrah without a mahram under current rules.
  • Visitors on a tourist eVisa can stay up to 90 days per visit. Operator-issued Umrah visas usually allow a shorter stay.
  • Family visit visa holders can perform Umrah too. See our Family Visit Visa guide.

Official Sources

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a special Umrah visa?
Usually not. Residents only need a Nusuk permit. Visitors can use a tourist eVisa, a visa on arrival or a family visit visa. A dedicated Umrah visa through a licensed operator is mainly for nationalities outside the eVisa list.
Can iqama holders perform Umrah?
Yes. Book a free Umrah permit in the Nusuk app with your iqama details. No visa is needed.
Is the Nusuk permit free?
Yes. The Umrah permit and the Rawdah permit are free, but you need them on top of your visa or iqama.
How long can I stay on a tourist eVisa?
The tourist eVisa is valid for one year with multiple entries, and each visit can last up to 90 days.
Can women perform Umrah without a mahram?
Yes. Under current Saudi rules women may travel for Umrah without a male guardian.
When is Umrah not possible?
For several weeks around the Hajj season, when Umrah permits and Umrah visas pause. The exact dates are announced each year on Nusuk.
