### Thread 1: The resignation cliff (pin this)

1/
Thinking of resigning in Saudi Arabia? Check your service date first.

Resigning 1 month before your 5-year mark can cost you half your end-of-service. 🧵

2/
EOS (Article 84):
• ½ month's wage per year for the first 5 years
• 1 month's wage per year after that

If you resign (Art. 85), you get a share:
• <2 yrs → 0
• 2–5 → 1/3
• 5–10 → 2/3
• 10+ → full

3/
On a SAR 10,000 wage:

4y 11m → ~SAR 8,194
5y 0m → ~SAR 16,667

9y 11m → ~SAR 49,444
10y 0m → SAR 75,000

One month. Up to SAR 25k difference.

4/
Also: "wage" ≠ basic only. HRSD defines it as basic + contractual allowances (e.g. fixed housing). Ask HR in writing which base they're using.

5/

### Thread 2: Final settlement checklist

1/ Leaving your job in KSA? Your final settlement is 5 separate things. Most people only check one. 🧵
2/ ① End of service: ½ month/yr (first 5), 1 month/yr after. Reduced if you resign.
3/ ② Unused annual leave paid in cash: (monthly wage ÷ 30) × days left. You earn 21 days/yr, 30 after 5 yrs.
4/ ③ Salary for days worked in your final month. ④ Notice pay if you weren't allowed to work it. ⑤ Ticket, if your contract includes one.
5/ The employer must pay within 1 week of contract end (2 weeks if you resigned). Add it all up here, free: https://www.saudiutilityhub.com/final-settlement-calculator.html?utm_source=x&utm_medium=social

### LinkedIn article (publish as a full article; about 900 words)

**Title:** End-of-Service Benefits in Saudi Arabia: A Payroll Reference Table for 2026

**Body:**

> End-of-service benefit (EOSB) is the single largest liability on most Saudi payrolls, and it's also the most disputed item when an employee leaves. Disputes almost always come down to one of three questions: which wage is the base, whether the exit is a resignation or a termination, and how partial years are counted.
>
> Below is a reference for HR and payroll teams, built directly from Articles 84 and 85 of the Labour Law as published by HRSD.
>
> **1. The formula**
>
> Article 84 grants half a month's wage for each of the first five years of service and one month's wage for each year after that. Partial years are pro-rated. The award is based on the *last* wage.
>
> Article 85 reduces the award when the worker resigns:
>
> | Service at resignation | Share |
> |---|---|
> | Under 2 years | None |
> | 2 to under 5 | One-third |
> | 5 to under 10 | Two-thirds |
> | 10 years or more | Full |
>
> **2. The wage base: the most common source of disputes**
>
> Many payroll systems still compute EOSB on basic salary alone. HRSD's guidance defines the wage as basic wage plus other amounts due under the contract, and permits excluding only commissions, percentages and similar components that genuinely vary, and only where the parties have agreed to exclude them. HRSD's own calculator asks for "actual wage".
>
> In practice, a fixed monthly housing or transport allowance written into the contract is likely to be treated as part of the wage. If your policy uses basic only, it's worth reviewing against current contracts before a dispute forces the issue.
>
> **3. Resignation vs contract expiry**
>
> A fixed-term contract that reaches its end date and isn't renewed is not a resignation. The employee is entitled to 100% under Article 84. Mislabelling expiries as resignations underpays employees and creates liability.
>
> **4. The cliffs, and why they matter for workforce planning**
>
> Because the resignation share jumps at 2, 5 and 10 years, employees close to those dates have a strong financial reason to stay a few more weeks. On a SAR 10,000 wage, resigning at 4 years 11 months pays about SAR 8,194, while resigning at 5 years pays about SAR 16,667. At 10 years the jump is about SAR 25,500. Expect notice letters to cluster just after those anniversaries.
>
> **5. Reference table: termination / contract end (SAR)**
>
> | Wage | 2 yrs | 5 yrs | 10 yrs | 20 yrs |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 5,000 | 5,000 | 12,500 | 37,500 | 87,500 |
> | 10,000 | 10,000 | 25,000 | 75,000 | 175,000 |
> | 20,000 | 20,000 | 50,000 | 150,000 | 350,000 |
>
> The full dataset (8 salary bands × 9 service lengths, termination and resignation, CSV download) is published here: https://www.saudiutilityhub.com/eos-payouts-by-salary-2026.html?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=article
>
> **6. What EOSB does not include**
>
> Unused annual leave (paid separately at the daily wage), salary for days worked, notice-period compensation, Article 77 compensation for unlawful termination, and any contractual ticket. An Article 80 dismissal removes the award entirely.
>
> **7. Payment timing**
>
> The employer must settle within one week of the contract ending, or within two weeks where the employee resigned.
>
> **8. Accrual tip for finance teams**
>
> Accrue monthly on the same wage base you would pay out on. Underaccruing on basic-only creates a gap that shows up all at once when a long-serving employee leaves.
>

### LinkedIn post 1 (short)

> HR question I get most often: "Is end of service calculated on basic or gross?"
>
> HRSD's answer: on the **wage**, which it defines as basic plus other contractual amounts. Only genuinely variable pay (commissions, percentages) can be excluded, and only by agreement.
>
> If your EOSB accrual runs on basic only, you're probably under-accruing.
