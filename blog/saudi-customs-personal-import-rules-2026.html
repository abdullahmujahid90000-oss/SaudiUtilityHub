Saudi Customs Rules 2026 — Duty-Free Allowance & Personal Import Guide for Expats

AA
Abdullah Al-Qahtani Founder & Editor, Saudi Utility Hub · Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
⚡ Quick Answer

Personal luggage and gifts up to SAR 3,000 in value are duty-free for any traveler. If you're moving to Saudi Arabia to live for the first time, your used personal belongings and household appliances are separately exempt from customs duty as long as they arrive within six months of your arrival and you can prove it's your first residence in the Kingdom. Online orders and courier parcels worth SAR 1,000 or less are also duty-free (a flat SAR 15 processing fee still applies). Alcohol, pork products, narcotics, and weapons remain completely banned, and cash or valuables worth SAR 60,000+ must be declared.

The Basic Duty-Free Traveler Allowance (SAR 3,000)

Every traveler entering Saudi Arabia — resident or visitor — can bring personal luggage and gifts worth up to SAR 3,000 without paying customs duty. This comes from Article 103(B) of the GCC Common Customs Law, and Saudi Arabia's customs authority (ZATCA) sets out the conditions clearly:

  • The value of the goods must not exceed SAR 3,000 (or the equivalent in another currency).
  • The items must be personal in nature and non-commercial in quantity — this isn't a wholesale-import allowance.
  • The goods must not appear on Saudi Arabia's prohibited or restricted items lists.
  • You shouldn't be a frequent traveler bringing in goods on a regular, repeat basis — customs treats that pattern as commercial activity.
  • Be ready to show your passport, national ID, or Iqama, plus receipts if you have them.

Separately, duty-free markets in arrival lounges at Saudi airports, seaports, and land borders let you buy up to SAR 3,000 of goods (with a 200-cigarette cap) directly on arrival, under rules ZATCA approved in 2024.

Moving to Saudi Arabia for the First Time: The Household Goods Exemption

This is the one that matters most for new expat arrivals. Separately from the SAR 3,000 traveler allowance, ZATCA exempts used personal luggage and household appliances from customs duty when you're relocating to Saudi Arabia to live — whether you're a citizen who was living abroad or a foreigner arriving to reside in the Kingdom for the first time. The official conditions are:

  • You must be a resident who was living abroad, or a foreigner arriving to reside in Saudi Arabia for the first time.
  • The items must be for genuine personal use and shipped from a place eligible for the exemption.
  • Foreigners must be able to prove this is their first-time arrival to reside in the country.
  • Your personal luggage and household appliances must arrive within six months of your own arrival date.
  • The exemption does not cover imported cars — vehicles go through a separate process (see below).
  • If any condition isn't met, the shipment becomes liable for duty under the normal customs tariff.

Required documentation typically includes a copy of your passport/national ID/Iqama, proof of your first-time arrival, receipts for new items, and — depending on how the shipment travels — a bill of lading (sea freight), a delivery order (air/sea freight, from your cargo agent), or a cargo manifest (land freight).

⚠️ Timing matters: The six-month window is measured from your own arrival date, not from when you place a shipping order. If you're planning a household move, start the shipping process as early as possible so the goods clear customs well inside the window — don't let paperwork delays push you past six months and turn a free shipment into a dutiable one.

Ordering Online: Low-Value Parcel Rules

If you're shipping personal items to Saudi Arabia by post or courier rather than moving your whole household, a separate — and simpler — threshold applies. Personal shipments valued at SAR 1,000 or less are exempt from customs duty. Since ZATCA's October 2024 customs services fee update, a flat SAR 15 customs declaration processing fee applies to these low-value shipments regardless of the duty exemption. Parcels valued above SAR 1,000 are assessed for duty and VAT in the normal way, based on the item's customs tariff classification.

What Happens If You Go Over the Limit?

Goods above the relevant exemption threshold are assessed under Saudi Arabia's standard customs schedule. The general customs duty rate under the GCC Common External Tariff is 5% of the goods' CIF value (Cost, Insurance, and Freight) for most product categories, though the exact rate depends on the item's specific tariff classification — you can look up the rate for a specific product using ZATCA's online customs tariff search. On top of any duty owed, Saudi Arabia's standard 15% VAT applies to the CIF value plus the duty amount.

A few product categories carry additional excise tax on top of ordinary customs duty and VAT — this is a separate tax under Saudi Arabia's Excise Tax Law, not something unique to travelers. As of ZATCA's current schedule, energy drinks are taxed at 100%, tobacco products are taxed heavily under Chapter 24 of the GCC tariff, and sweetened beverages were moved in January 2026 to a tiered system based on sugar content (rather than a flat rate), with plain carbonated water and 100% natural juice excluded.

Prohibited & Restricted Items

Regardless of value, some goods cannot enter Saudi Arabia under any exemption. Items that are absolutely prohibited include alcohol and any product containing alcohol intended for consumption, pork and pork-derived products, narcotics, and weapons. A separate, longer list of restricted items — including certain publications and religious materials, wireless/radio-controlled equipment, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, and some alcohol-containing non-consumable products like perfume — requires prior special approval from the relevant Saudi authority before you can bring them in.

Bringing in prohibited items, or restricted items without the required approval, can result in confiscation, a customs fine, and referral to the Customs Court, which can order fines, seizure, or imprisonment — doubled for repeat violations. Always check ZATCA's current prohibited and restricted goods lists before packing anything you're unsure about.

Declaring Cash and Valuables (SAR 60,000+)

Separately from goods, Saudi Arabia requires travelers to declare cash, monetary instruments, gold bullion, precious metals, precious stones, or fine jewelry worth SAR 60,000 or more, under Saudi Arabia's Anti-Money Laundering Law. You can complete the declaration manually on arrival/departure or electronically via the Saudi Customs website or app before you travel.

⚠️ Penalties for not declaring: Failing to declare cash or valuables at or above the SAR 60,000 threshold triggers a fine of 25% of the seized value for a first violation and 50% for a repeat violation. If a link to money laundering or another crime is suspected, the full amount can be seized and the case referred to the Public Prosecution, which can order confiscation and imprisonment.

Bringing a Personal Vehicle

Importing a car is handled separately from household goods and is not covered by the six-month personal-effects exemption. ZATCA runs a "Vehicle Import for Individuals" self-service e-service, open to both citizens and residents, that lets you complete the import declaration online without a customs broker — after registering the vehicle's chassis number and details, uploading the required documents (export certificate/ownership document plus a bill of lading for sea freight, or an import declaration for land ports), and paying the applicable duty, VAT, and fees through SADAD, mada, or a credit card. Vehicles that have traveled more than 1,000 km, or whose data isn't already registered with the Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization (SASO), must pass an inspection at an accredited center, and additional fees can apply if the vehicle doesn't meet age or efficiency criteria.

Personal vehicle import quotas and exact fee amounts are set by ZATCA and can change, so confirm the current rules and any per-person quota directly on the ZATCA portal or through the service's official user manual before shipping a car.

Before You Ship: A Simple Checklist

  1. Confirm your arrival status Know your exact arrival date in Saudi Arabia — the 6-month household-goods window is measured from it, and you'll need to prove it's your first time residing in the Kingdom.
  2. Separate personal items from anything commercial-looking Duplicate items, bulk quantities, or unopened retail goods can be treated as commercial and lose the exemption.
  3. Check every item against the prohibited/restricted lists This includes less obvious things — some supplements, alcohol-based perfumes, and religious materials fall under restricted categories that need prior approval.
  4. Keep documentation together Passport/Iqama copy, receipts, bill of lading or delivery order, and proof of first-time arrival — have these ready before your shipment reaches a Saudi port.
  5. Declare anything over SAR 60,000 in cash or valuables Do this even if you're confident it's legitimate — the declaration itself is the requirement, not just having clean funds.

Common Questions About Saudi Customs Rules

How much can I bring into Saudi Arabia duty-free? ▼
Under Article 103(B) of the GCC Common Customs Law, personal luggage and gifts carried by a traveler are exempt from customs duty as long as their value does not exceed SAR 3,000, they are non-commercial in nature and quantity, and they are not on Saudi Arabia's prohibited or restricted goods lists.
Can I bring my furniture and household items when I move to Saudi Arabia for the first time? ▼
Yes. ZATCA exempts used personal luggage and household appliances from customs duty for a resident living abroad or a foreigner coming to reside in Saudi Arabia for the first time, provided the items are for personal use, you can prove you are arriving to reside for the first time, and the shipment arrives within six months of your arrival date. The exemption does not cover imported cars, and if any condition isn't met the shipment becomes liable for duty under the standard tariff.
Do I pay customs duty on online orders shipped to Saudi Arabia? ▼
Personal parcels valued at SAR 1,000 or less arriving by post or courier are exempt from customs duty, though a flat SAR 15 customs declaration processing fee applies per shipment as of ZATCA's October 2024 fee structure. Parcels above SAR 1,000 are assessed for duty and 15% VAT in the normal way.
What items are banned from being brought into Saudi Arabia? ▼
Alcohol, pork and pork products, narcotics, and weapons are prohibited outright. A wider list of restricted items — including certain publications, religious materials, wireless equipment, and products containing alcohol such as some perfumes — require prior approval from the relevant Saudi authority before import. Always check ZATCA's current prohibited and restricted goods lists before shipping or traveling with borderline items.
Do I need to declare cash when entering or leaving Saudi Arabia? ▼
Yes. Travelers carrying cash, monetary instruments, gold, precious metals or fine jewelry worth SAR 60,000 or more must declare it under Saudi Arabia's Anti-Money Laundering Law. Failing to declare an amount at or above this threshold can result in a fine of 25% of the seized value for a first violation, 50% for a repeat violation, or full seizure and referral to the Public Prosecution in serious cases.
