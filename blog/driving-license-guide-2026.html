Driving License for Expats in Saudi Arabia 2026 — Exchange, Renewal & Requirements

AA
Abdullah Al-Qahtani, Founder & Editor Independently operated · reviewed against official Saudi government sources
⚡ Quick Answer

Expats get a Saudi driving license one of two ways: a license exchange (no driving test) if your home country is on the traffic authority's approved list, or the full driving-school route (theory + practical test) if it isn't. Both paths run through Absher. Once you hold a Saudi license, you can renew it online up to 180 days before expiry, choosing a 2, 5, or 10-year validity and passing a basic eye test. Renewing late costs SAR 100 per year or part-year (max SAR 300) after a 60-day grace period. A valid Iqama is required for any of this to go through.

Who Needs a Saudi Driving License?

If you are living in Saudi Arabia as a resident (Iqama holder), you are expected to hold a valid Saudi driving license to drive legally — a foreign license on its own is generally only recognized for short-term visitors, not residents. Visitors on a tourist visa can typically drive a rental car using a valid foreign license for the length of their visa, and an International Driving Permit (IDP) is strongly recommended — and effectively required if your home license isn't printed in Latin script — since it provides an official translation that rental counters and traffic police can read.

The General Directorate of Traffic (Muroor), under the Ministry of Interior, is the authority that issues, exchanges, and renews driving licenses. In practice, almost every step — booking an appointment, uploading documents, paying fees, checking status — happens through the Absher platform rather than by visiting a traffic office in person.

Two Ways Expats Get a Saudi License

1. License Exchange (No Driving Test)

If your existing driving license was issued by a country on Saudi Arabia's approved exchange list, you can convert it to a Saudi license without sitting a driving test. This list commonly includes the GCC states, the United States, the United Kingdom, most EU countries, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand, among others — but the exact list is maintained and updated by the General Directorate of Traffic and can change, so always check your own country's current status inside your Absher account rather than relying on a general list you find online.

Typical documents requested for an exchange include:

  • Your original foreign driving license (front and back)
  • An official Arabic translation of that license (some translation offices note it should be authenticated to the standards the traffic authority expects)
  • A copy of your Iqama and passport
  • Recent passport-size photos
  • A basic medical/eye examination, arranged through an authorized center
⚠️ Don't assume — verify your country's status first. The eligible-country list changes periodically and third-party blogs are not always current. Check the list inside your own Absher account, or confirm with the General Directorate of Traffic, before booking translations or appointments.

2. Full Driving-School Route (Theory + Practical Test)

If your home country isn't on the exchange list, you'll need to go through Saudi Arabia's standard licensing process: theory lessons, a computer-based theory exam, practical driving lessons at a licensed school, and a practical road test. This route takes longer and costs more than an exchange, but it's the only path available if exchange eligibility doesn't apply to you. Driving schools can advise on current lesson packages and test scheduling.

Renewing a Saudi Driving License via Absher

Once you hold a Saudi license, renewal is handled online:

  1. Check your renewal window Absher allows renewal up to 180 days before your current license expires — you don't need to wait until the last minute.
  2. Log in to Absher and open the driving license renewal service Under "My Services" → "Traffic," select the driving license renewal option.
  3. Complete a recent eye test An eye test at an authorized optical or medical center is required as part of the renewal.
  4. Choose your new validity period Pick 2, 5, or 10 years — the fee scales with the length you choose.
  5. Confirm your National Address and pay online Your renewed license (or physical card, where applicable) is linked to your registered National Address; payment is made by Mada, Visa, or Mastercard.
💡 Tip: Make sure your Iqama is valid before starting a renewal or exchange. If your residency has lapsed, driving-license transactions on Absher won't go through until it's sorted out — use our Iqama Expiry Tracker to check your status first.

Late Renewal — What It Costs

If you let your license lapse, Saudi traffic regulations apply a fine of SAR 100 for each year or part of a year the license remains unrenewed, up to a maximum of SAR 300. This fine only applies after a 60-day grace period from the expiry date — so a short delay right after expiry shouldn't trigger it, but pushing renewal off for months will. Beyond the fine itself, driving on an expired license can also be treated as a separate traffic violation if you're stopped, so it's best not to let it run out for long.

Traffic Violations and the Points System — A Quick Overview

Saudi Arabia's Traffic Law assigns a specific number of points to each violation depending on its severity, and these points accumulate on the driver's record; a license can be revoked if the accumulated points exceed the threshold set in the law's regulations. Violators generally have the right to appeal a recorded violation before the competent court within 30 days of being notified, and in many cases a fine can be reduced by up to 25% if settled promptly. Because exact fine amounts and point values are set out in the law's detailed violation schedule and are periodically updated, this guide intentionally doesn't reproduce a fine-by-fine table — check current amounts for a specific violation directly through your Absher account (Services → Inquiries → Traffic) before assuming any figure you see elsewhere is still accurate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can any expat exchange their foreign driving license for a Saudi one? ▼
Only if your license was issued by a country on the General Directorate of Traffic's approved exchange list, which commonly includes the GCC states, the US, UK, most EU countries, Canada, Australia and New Zealand among others. If your country isn't on the list, you must complete Saudi Arabia's full driving-school process instead: theory lessons, a computer-based theory test, practical lessons, and a practical road test. The list is maintained by the traffic authority and can change, so always confirm your specific country's current status inside your Absher account before making any plans.
Can I drive in Saudi Arabia on my home country's license without exchanging it? ▼
Only as a short-term visitor — for example on a tourist visa, using a valid foreign license (an International Driving Permit is strongly recommended, and required if your license isn't in Latin script) generally lets you drive a rental car for the length of your visa. Once you take up residency in Saudi Arabia on an Iqama, you are expected to hold a valid Saudi driving license to drive legally; you cannot rely on a foreign license indefinitely as a resident.
How long before expiry can I renew my Saudi driving license? ▼
Absher's online renewal service allows renewal up to 180 days before your current license expires. During renewal you choose a new validity period of 2, 5 or 10 years and pay the fee by card; a recent eye test from an authorized optical/medical center is required as part of the process.
What happens if my Saudi driving license expires and I don't renew it on time? ▼
Saudi traffic regulations apply a late-renewal fine of SAR 100 for each year or part of a year the license has lapsed, capped at SAR 300, and this fine only kicks in after a 60-day grace period following the expiry date. Driving on an expired license beyond that grace period also risks a separate traffic violation and complications at checkpoints, so it's best to renew before or shortly after expiry.
Does my Iqama need to be valid to renew or exchange a driving license? ▼
Yes. Your residency status needs to be in order for driving-license transactions to go through on Absher, so if your Iqama has lapsed you should renew it first. Use our Iqama Expiry Tracker to check your status before starting a driving-license application.
