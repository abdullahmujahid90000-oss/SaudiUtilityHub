🪪 The 5-Year Iqama Card: Why "Valid Until 2029" Can Still Get You Fined

The date printed on your card and the date your residency actually expires are two different things. Here's how to tell them apart, and the 30-second Absher check that keeps you out of trouble.

Home › 5-Year Resident ID Guide

You glance at your iqama card: it says it's valid for years. Then HR messages you that your iqama expires next month. Both are true, and the difference between them is why people get fined, stopped at the airport or have bank accounts frozen.

Short answer: the five years apply to the plastic Resident ID card (هوية مقيم), not to your right to live in Saudi Arabia. Your residency is renewed electronically for the period you or your employer pays for, usually 3, 6, 9 or 12 months at a time. One card can stay in your wallet for five years while the iqama behind it is renewed many times.

This isn't new either. Jawazat introduced the five-year card at the start of the Hijri year 1437, in October 2015, when it replaced the old residence booklet. Posts calling it a "new 2026 rule" are recycling old news.

The Card vs Your Residency

Resident ID cardResidency (iqama) validity
What it isThe physical card you carryYour legal permission to live in the Kingdom
How long it lastsFive years from issueThe period paid at renewal, usually 3 to 12 months
Where you see the datePrinted on the cardIn Absher, or your employer's Muqeem account
When it endsA new card is printedRenewal fees and levies must be paid, or late fines start

The golden rule: never judge your status by the card. Check the residency date in Absher before you book a flight, apply for a visa, open a bank account or sign a lease.

Find Your Real Expiry Date in 30 Seconds

  1. Open the Absher Individuals app and sign in.
  2. Tap your profile, or open Digital Documents and choose the Digital Resident ID.
  3. The residency expiry date is shown in both Hijri and Gregorian. The Hijri date is the one the system works from.

No Absher access? Your HR team can see the same date in Muqeem. Then put it into our Iqama Expiry Planner to count the days left and choose when to start the renewal.

Why Employers Renew for 3 Months Instead of 12

The iqama renewal bill isn't just a card fee. It includes the iqama fee and, for most private-sector workers, the SAR 800 monthly work permit levy, all paid for the period being renewed. Paying three months at a time spreads that cost for the employer. It's normal and nothing to worry about.

What it means for you is simply that your expiry date comes round more often. If you're planning a trip home, check the date in Absher before you request an exit re-entry visa. The return date can't sensibly run past a residency that's about to lapse. Our Iqama Renewal Fees page breaks down what each renewal period costs.

Your Digital Iqama Is Usually Enough

Since January 2021 every resident with Absher has had a Digital Resident ID in the Absher Individuals app. It shows your details with a changing QR code and can be saved for offline use. Police checkpoints, banks, clinics and most offices accept it, so leaving the card at home is rarely a disaster.

Keep the plastic card for travel and for the odd office that still insists on it, and make sure your phone is charged when you rely on the digital one.

Lost, Damaged or Wrong Details?

  • Lost or stolen: report it straight away through Absher's lost iqama service, or at Jawazat. Jawazat's fine for losing a resident ID is SAR 1,000, plus the fee for a replacement card. Several sources say repeat losses cost more, but no official schedule for that is published.
  • Damaged or worn out: request a replacement, usually for SAR 500. The new card is marked as a replacement and keeps the original dates.
  • Wrong details: ask your employer to correct them through Muqeem. For dependants, the sponsor handles it through Absher or Jawazat.

Workers' cards are normally printed by the employer. Dependants' cards are handled by the sponsor.

Real-Life Example: Ravi's "Valid" Card

Ravi, a site engineer in Dammam, books flights home for his sister's wedding. His card says valid until 2029, so he doesn't think twice. Two days before the trip, his exit re-entry request won't go through, because his residency expired three weeks earlier. His company had renewed for six months and missed the date.

HR renews it the same day and pays the SAR 500 late fine. Ravi makes his flight, just. Since then he checks Absher on the first of every month, and he's set a reminder 30 days before each expiry date.

Mistakes People Make

  • Trusting the printed date. It's the card's date, not your residency's.
  • Booking travel without checking Absher. An expired residency blocks exit re-entry visas.
  • Assuming HR will remember. Late renewal is fined SAR 500 the first time and SAR 1,000 the second.
  • Not reporting a lost card. Report it quickly so nobody can misuse it, and you'll pay the standard fine rather than face bigger problems.
  • Forgetting the family. Dependants' residency runs on the same cycle as yours, and the SAR 400 monthly dependent fee is paid for the same period.

Family Members

Your spouse and children get their own Resident ID cards under your sponsorship. Their residency is renewed together with yours, and their monthly dependant fee is paid for the same period. If your iqama lapses, theirs does too. That's one more reason to keep an eye on the date in Absher.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my iqama valid for five years?
The card is, but your residency isn't. Residency is renewed electronically for the period paid at renewal, usually 3 to 12 months. Check the real expiry date in Absher.
Is the five-year Resident ID new in 2026?
No. Jawazat introduced it in October 2015, at the start of the Hijri year 1437.
Where can I see my iqama expiry date?
In the Absher Individuals app, on your Digital Resident ID or profile. Your employer can also see it in Muqeem.
Can I use the digital iqama instead of the card?
In most everyday situations, yes. The Digital Resident ID in Absher is widely accepted as proof of identity inside Saudi Arabia.
What is the fine for losing my iqama?
SAR 1,000 for a lost resident ID, according to Jawazat, plus the fee for a replacement card shown in Absher.
Do I need a new card every time I renew my iqama?
No. The card stays the same for its five years. Only the residency validity in the system changes when you renew.
