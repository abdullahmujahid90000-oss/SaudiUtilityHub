Saudi Five-Year Resident ID: Card vs Electronic Residency

Independent guide · Not affiliated with Jawazat, MOI, Absher, or Muqeem · Last reviewed: 27 July 2026

✅ Concise Answer

Saudi Arabia's physical "هوية مقيم" (Resident ID) card is commonly described as usable for five years — but this is not a new 2026 feature. We independently verified, directly against a Saudi Press Agency article, that this card replaced the earlier residence-permit document starting around 1/1/1437H, corresponding to approximately October 2015. Separately, the underlying electronic residency record still needs periodic renewal through the official service — the two are not the same thing, and mixing them up is the main source of confusion this page addresses.

⚠️ Correction to a previous version of this page: an earlier version of this guide incorrectly described the five-year Resident ID as a "new 2026 rollout" automatically given to new arrivals from January 2026 or to existing residents "at their next renewal" as if it were a recent change. That framing was inaccurate. Independent research directly against a Saudi Press Agency (SPA) article confirms the card system dates to approximately 2015 (1/1/1437H) — roughly a decade before 2026. This page has been rebuilt to correct that error.

4️⃣ What the Five-Year Period Actually Means

Based on the SPA article we fetched directly, the five-year period was introduced as the validity of the physical card document — replacing an older system where a physical card had to be reissued more frequently. It is not, on the evidence we found, a statement that your legal residency itself is guaranteed for five years without any further action.

5️⃣ What Still Requires Periodic Electronic Renewal

Your electronic residency record is described across sources as needing periodic renewal separate from the physical card's five-year period. We found this described inconsistently — most commonly as annual, though the research brief for this page notes that some official material references one- or two-year periods. We did not find one single primary source reconciling these into a definitive current rule, so we do not state a specific renewal interval as guaranteed fact here. Check the renewal requirement and any associated fee directly on the official service that manages your residency.

6️⃣ Dates Residents Commonly Confuse

  • The physical Resident ID card's own five-year period, vs. the electronic residency record's separate renewal cycle
  • Iqama/residency status, vs. an exit/re-entry visa's own validity window
  • Resident ID validity, vs. passport expiry — these are entirely separate documents from different authorities

9️⃣ What to Check on the Physical Card

Secondary Saudi financial-press coverage reports that the redesigned Resident ID does not print a conventional expiry date in the way the older document did. We were unable to independently re-confirm the exact wording of this from a primary Jawazat source, so we present it here as a reported claim rather than a confirmed fact — check your own physical card and the official electronic service directly rather than relying on this description.

🔟 Digital Resident ID in Absher

This is one of the more solidly confirmed points in this guide. We fetched a Saudi Press Agency article directly: the General Directorate of Passports launched a digital version of the Resident ID inside the Ministry of Interior's "Absher Individuals" app, called "هوية مقيم الرقمية" (Digital Resident ID). It lets a resident display their identity data electronically via a changing QR code, and can be downloaded to a smartphone for offline viewing without needing an internet connection. This was announced 6 January 2021; we found no primary indication it has been discontinued, but we did not separately re-confirm it is still live in July 2026.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Is the five-year Resident ID a new 2026 rollout?+
No. We independently verified this directly against a Saudi Press Agency article and other coverage: the هوية مقيم (Resident ID) card, valid for five years, replaced the earlier residence-permit document starting around 1/1/1437H, corresponding to approximately October 2015 — roughly a decade before 2026. This page previously stated a 2026 launch; that was incorrect and has been corrected.
If the card lasts five years, why do I still hear about annual renewal?+
Because two different things are involved. The five-year period is commonly described as applying to the physical card itself. Your underlying electronic residency record is a separate thing that still requires periodic renewal through the official service — sources describe this cadence inconsistently (annual in most descriptions, with some official material referencing different periods), so we do not state a single guaranteed renewal interval here. Check the current renewal requirement and fee directly on the official service for your case.
Does the physical card show an expiry date?+
Secondary Saudi financial-press coverage has reported that it does not print a conventional expiry date in the way the older document did, attributed to a Jawazat statement. We could not independently re-confirm the exact wording of this from a primary source, so we present it as a reported claim rather than a confirmed fact — check your own card directly.
Is "Muqeem Card" an official name for this ID?+
We found no primary source confirming "Muqeem Card" as an official English name, or confirming that the card is called this because of the Muqeem platform. The Arabic official term reported in our primary sources is "هوية مقيم" (Resident ID). We do not repeat the "Muqeem Card" naming explanation from the previous version of this page.
Does the card have a biometric chip that can be tapped at airports or hospitals?+
We found no primary source confirming a contactless chip, fingerprint or facial-template storage, or tap-to-use functionality at airports, hospitals, or government counters. These claims from the previous version of this page could not be verified and have been removed rather than restated as fact.
What fee applies to renew my residency or replace my card?+
We did not find one primary-confirmed, universal fee figure that applies to every category of resident. Secondary sources report different amounts for different categories (sponsored workers, domestic workers, dependants, parents), which we could not independently verify. This guide does not state a specific fee — check the amount shown by the official service for your own case.
Does this page calculate when my card or residency expires?+
No. This page intentionally does not calculate a five-year card-replacement date, a residency-validity date, a penalty, or a fee. The interactive tool on this page only helps you identify which type of date you are looking at and where to verify it — it does not produce a status result.
