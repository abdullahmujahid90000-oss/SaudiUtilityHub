The Five-Year Resident ID (Iqama Card) Explained

Why the card lasts five years while your iqama is renewed every few months, and how to check the date that actually matters.

Card vs residencyAbsher stepsUpdated October 2026
Home › 5-Year Resident ID Guide

Short answer: the five-year period applies to the plastic Resident ID card (هوية مقيم), not to your right to live in Saudi Arabia. Your residency is still renewed electronically for the period you or your employer pays for, usually 3, 6, 9 or 12 months at a time. The card can stay in your wallet for five years while the iqama behind it is renewed several times.

This confuses a lot of people, partly because many posts online describe the five-year card as something new. It is not. Jawazat introduced it at the start of the Hijri year 1437, in October 2015, replacing the old residence permit booklet.

Card vs Residency: The Difference

Resident ID cardResidency (iqama) validity
What it isThe physical card you carryYour legal permission to live in the Kingdom
How long it lastsFive years from issueThe period paid at renewal (3 to 12 months for most workers)
Where you see the dateOn the cardIn Absher or your employer's Muqeem account
What happens when it endsA new card is printedRenewal fees and levies must be paid, or fines apply

The practical lesson: never judge your status by the card. Check the residency expiry date in Absher before you travel, apply for a visa, or open a bank account.

How to Check Your Real Iqama Expiry Date

  1. Open the Absher Individuals app and log in.
  2. Tap your profile photo, or open the Digital Documents section and choose the Digital Resident ID.
  3. The residency expiry date is shown there in both Hijri and Gregorian. The Hijri date is the one the system uses.

If you do not have Absher access, your employer's HR team can check it in Muqeem. Our Iqama Expiry Planner helps you work out how many days you have left and when to start the renewal.

The Digital Resident ID in Absher

Since January 2021, every resident with Absher has a digital version of the Resident ID inside the Absher Individuals app. It shows your details with a changing QR code and can be saved for offline use. In daily life it is accepted in most places that ask for your iqama, such as police checkpoints, banks and clinics, so a forgotten card at home is rarely a problem.

Lost or Damaged Card

  • Lost: report it as soon as possible through Absher (the lost iqama reporting service) or at Jawazat. A fine of SAR 1,000 applies for a first loss, and it goes up if it happens again. A replacement fee is also charged, and the amount is shown in Absher before you pay.
  • Damaged or worn out: a replacement card can be requested, usually for a fee of SAR 500. Bring the damaged card with you.
  • Wrong details on the card: ask your employer to correct the data through Muqeem, or go to Jawazat if you are a dependent.

For workers, the employer normally handles printing the new card. For dependents, the sponsor does it through Absher.

Family Members

Your wife, children and other dependents get their own Resident ID cards under your sponsorship. Their residency is renewed together with yours, and the SAR 400 monthly dependent fee is paid for the same period. See the dependent fee calculator to see what that costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my iqama valid for five years?
The card is, but your residency is not. Residency is renewed electronically for the period paid at renewal, usually between 3 and 12 months. Check the real expiry date in Absher.
Is the five-year Resident ID new in 2026?
No. Jawazat introduced it in October 2015, at the start of the Hijri year 1437.
Where can I see my iqama expiry date?
In the Absher Individuals app, on your Digital Resident ID or profile. Your employer can also see it in Muqeem.
Can I use the digital iqama instead of the card?
In most everyday situations, yes. The Digital Resident ID in Absher is widely accepted as proof of identity inside Saudi Arabia.
What is the fine for losing my iqama?
SAR 1,000 for a first loss, plus a replacement fee shown in Absher. Repeat losses are fined more.
Do I need a new card every time I renew my iqama?
No. The card stays the same for five years. Only the residency validity in the system changes when you renew.
